# The Rye From Lawrenceburg

Research outline: one Indiana distillery and the American rye revival

_This is the other half of the speaker notes. It does not tell you what to say; it tells you where to look, what is settled, what is still moving, and which widely repeated claims will trip you up. Facts were checked in November 2026. Which brands buy from which distillery changes quickly, so re-check anything about a current bottle against its label._

> **THE SPINE OF THE STORY**
> George Ross's rye distillery in Lawrenceburg, Indiana (1847) → bought by Seagram after Prohibition (1933) → for decades it makes high-rye whiskey to flavour blends, and a 95% rye, 5% malted barley recipe is settled on → after Seagram is broken up, Pernod Ricard plans to close it (2006) → sold to a Trinidad company and renamed Lawrenceburg Distillers Indiana (2007) → bought by MGP Ingredients (2011), which sells whiskey to other brands → the rye revival takes that 95% rye to Iowa, Tennessee, Kentucky, West Virginia, Utah and beyond → the distillery launches its own Rossville Union rye (2018) and takes back the name Ross & Squibb (2021) → several brands build their own distilleries, and MGP renames itself Sempreon (November 2026).

## How to use this

Each module gives you the question worth answering, the facts that are nailed down (with dates, so you can check them), the sources, threads worth pulling, and the traps. The history of this distillery is mostly told by trade press and whiskey writers rather than by archives, so where only one secondary source supports a claim, it is listed under **Not confirmed** at the end and said aloud as "the story goes" or "one account says".

## Module 1. Rossville Union

_Who started the distillery, and what was it before Seagram?_

### What's established

- **1847.** George Ross starts a rye distillery in Lawrenceburg, Indiana, which becomes the Rossville Union Distillery.
- The name returns twice: in MGP's Rossville Union rye brand (2018) and in Ross & Squibb Distillery, the name the plant took back in 2021.

### Go to the source

- › _Wikipedia, "MGP Ingredients": the 1847 founding, with citations._
- › _Fred Minnick, "MGP Launches Rye with Historical Roots: Rossville Union", 9 May 2018: how the company itself tells the founding story._
- › _Dearborn County histories and Indiana newspapers on Hoosier State Chronicles (free) and Chronicling America: the place to find George Ross himself._

### Threads worth pulling

- Almost nothing in the trade press says who George Ross was. A county history or a newspaper archive would make a good evening's digging, and the result would improve this talk.
- Where does "Squibb" come from? The 2021 rename honours a Prohibition-era name; trace it.

> **TRAPS**
> Do not call Rossville Union "the oldest distillery in Indiana" or give it a continuous history without checking: the site has changed hands, names and purpose many times, and Prohibition interrupted everything.

## Module 2. Seagram and the 95% rye

_Why did one plant make such an unusual rye, and who designed it?_

### What's established

- **1933.** Seagram buys the Lawrenceburg distillery after Prohibition.
- The plant's high-rye whiskey was made mainly as a flavouring component for blends such as **Seagram's 5 Crown and 7 Crown**.
- **Larry Ebersold**, who began distilling at Lawrenceburg in 1972, settled on **95% rye and 5% malted barley**. Accounts differ on when.
- Very high-rye mashes foam and are hard to process; one writer credits the Lawrenceburg equipment with handling them better than most.
- MGP's own product page today lists a 95% rye whiskey: rye 95%, barley malt 5%.

### Go to the source

- › _VinePair, "95/5 Rye: MGP's Signature to Craft Whiskey Staple": Ebersold and the blending purpose._
- › _Whiskey University, "Larry Ebersold": career profile._
- › _Bourbon Veach, "Some more thoughts on rye", 20 March 2017: the practical difficulty of high-rye mashes._
- › _MGP Ingredients, "95% Rye Whiskey" product page._
- › _Indiana Landmarks photograph, "Seagrams Distillers, Ridge Avenue (Lawrenceburg, Ind.)", via Wikimedia Commons and DPLA._

### Threads worth pulling

- Taste the rye against a modern American blended whiskey and ask what a few percent of it would do there. That is what the recipe was for.
- Difford's Guide's entry on High West's 12-year-old touches on Ebersold's experiments after 1983. Follow him: his career is the recipe's biography.

> **TRAPS**
> The date of the 95/5 recipe is genuinely uncertain: one source says Ebersold experimented after 1983, another the early 1990s. Don't give a single year.

## Module 3. Nearly closed

_How did the distillery go from Seagram workhorse to contract supplier?_

### What's established

- After Seagram's drinks business is broken up (around 2000–2001), the plant goes to **Pernod Ricard**.
- **19 April 2006.** Pernod Ricard announces it will close the distillery.
- **2007.** Instead, it is sold to **CL Financial**, a Trinidad and Tobago holding company, which renames it **Lawrenceburg Distillers Indiana (LDI)**. CL Financial collapses in the 2008–09 crisis and requires government intervention.
- **2011.** **MGP Ingredients** of Atchison, Kansas agrees in October 2011 to buy the distillery (sources put completion in December).

### Go to the source

- › _Wikipedia, "MGP Ingredients": the 2006 announcement and the 2011 agreement, with citations._
- › _The Bourbon Culture, "MGP Indiana: Past, Present and Future": the LDI years._
- › _Cincinnati Magazine, "The Biggest Distillery You've Never Heard of Is in Lawrenceburg, Indiana"._

> **TRAPS**
> "LDI" and "MGP" whiskey are the same plant at different dates. Older reviews say LDI; newer ones say MGP. Don't treat them as different distilleries.

## Module 4. The revival brands

_What did each brand do with the same rye?_

### What's established

- **Bulleit 95 Rye.** Introduced 2011; 95/5 recipe distilled at Lawrenceburg to Bulleit's specification; bottled in Kentucky; 90 proof; no age statement. Whisky Advocate (2026): Bulleit's Kentucky distilleries make bourbon, not rye, and all Bulleit ryes have been and will be the Indiana 95% recipe. About $25–33.
- **George Dickel Rye.** 90 proof; 95/5 from MGP; no age statement. Dickel says it is chilled and filtered through sugar-maple charcoal. Where: disputed (Tennessee, per retailers; Diageo's Plainfield, Illinois plant, per Wikipedia). About $22–30.
- **Redemption Rye.** 92 proof; 95/5 from MGP; Deutsch Family Wine & Spirits; open about its sourcing. About $25–30.
- **Smooth Ambler Old Scout.** Maxwelton, West Virginia; "Old Scout" marks its sourced whiskey; the straight rye is MGP 95/5 at 99 proof (current availability unclear).
- **High West Double Rye.** 92 proof; MGP 95/5 blended with High West's own 80% rye, 20% malted rye from Wanship, Utah; own share kept under half. Earlier versions used a 16-year-old Barton rye instead.
- **Angel's Envy Rye** and **Filibuster** are listed with Bulleit Rye and Dickel Rye as brands bottling the 95% rye in Wikipedia's MGP article.
- A 2021 trade report names Bulleit Rye, Dickel Rye and Templeton Rye among the plant's contract customers.

### Go to the source

- › _Whisky Advocate, "Bulleit Celebrates Rye With a 20 Year Old Release" (2026)._
- › _Breaking Bourbon, "George Dickel Rye Whiskey" review._
- › _Paste, "Finding Redemption" (April 2015); Beverage Dynamics, "The Redemption Rye guys talk wheat whiskey, young whiskey and sourcing" (March 2019)._
- › _Difford's Guide, "Smooth Ambler Old Scout 7 Year Old Straight Rye Whiskey"._
- › _Whisky Advocate, "High West Is Making a Big Change to Double Rye"._
- › _Beverage Dynamics, 1 October 2021, on the Ross & Squibb rename and the contract customers._

### Threads worth pulling

- Taste two of these blind against each other. Same recipe, same still: what is left to tell them apart is exactly what each brand chose.
- Dickel's charcoal step is the clearest example of a brand changing the spirit after buying it. Is the effect visible on a 95% rye?

> **TRAPS**
> These are the same recipe from the same distillery, not the same whiskey: barrels, age, proof, filtering and blending all differ. And brand lists go out of date fast; check a current bottle before you name it as Lawrenceburg rye.

## Module 5. Templeton

_A town's legend, a label, and a distillery of its own._

### What's established

- **The brand.** Founded 2006 in Templeton, Iowa, and marketed on a Prohibition-era Iowa rye recipe.
- **The whiskey.** Distilled and aged in Lawrenceburg, bottled in Iowa. In 2014 the Des Moines Register reported that a flavouring formulation from a Louisville flavour house was being added; federal rules allow small amounts of such blending materials in some whiskey (about 2.5% by volume), but the result cannot be labelled straight.
- **The settlement (2015).** Class actions in Cook County, Illinois settled in July 2015 (reported at about $2.5 million). Buyers between 1 January 2006 and 21 July 2015 could claim $6 a bottle with proof or $3 without, up to six bottles, one claim per household. The company agreed to add "Distilled in Indiana" to the label and to drop "Prohibition era recipe" and "small batch". Payments went out from March 2016.
- **The distillery.** Ground broken 5 April 2017; opened August 2018 (reported $35 million). Its first own-made release was a bourbon, Fortitude (2024).
- **Now.** Templeton Straight Rye, launched 7 November 2024: 95/5, at least four years old, 90 proof, $29.99 suggested; the release describes it as selected from sourced rye inventory. Templeton's first rye distilled, aged and bottled in Iowa, a limited single barrel, came out on 30 October 2025.

### Go to the source

- › _Top Class Actions, 16 July 2015, "Templeton Reaches Rye Whiskey Class Action Settlement"._
- › _Chuck Cowdery, "Templeton Tale Gets Curiouser and Curiouser", 3 October 2014._
- › _Wikipedia, "Templeton Rye"._
- › _Beverage Dynamics, 7 August 2018, "Templeton Rye opens new $35M distillery in Iowa"._
- › _Fred Minnick, 8 November 2024, "Templeton Rolls Out New Straight Rye Whiskey"._
- › _The Whiskey Wash, October 2025, "Templeton Launches First Single Barrel Rye from Iowa"._

### Threads worth pulling

- The Prohibition legend itself. Iowa newspapers of the 1920s are digitised; is there contemporary evidence of Templeton bootleggers?
- Compare a pre-2015 back label with a current one.

> **TRAPS**
> Tell the settlement plainly: it was a civil settlement over marketing, not a fine, and the reports found here do not record an admission of wrongdoing. And don't call Templeton either "all Indiana" or "all Iowa" in late 2026: it is both, depending on the bottle.

## Module 6. Reading the back label

_What does federal law require a whiskey label to say about where it was distilled?_

### What's established

- **27 CFR 5.66(f), state of distillation.** The state where original distillation took place must appear on the label of most types of American whisky (those in 27 CFR 5.143(c)(2)–(7), (15) and (16)) distilled in the United States. One permitted form puts the state with the type ("Kentucky bourbon whisky"), but only if the whisky was distilled and aged there. The regulator can require it even where the state appears in the name and address, to avoid a misleading impression.
- **TTB's label checklist** gives examples such as "Distilled in Idaho" and notes that the state must be shown where the whisky was not distilled in the state of the label address.
- The rule predates the Templeton case (it appears as 27 CFR 5.36 in the 2011 edition, with exemptions for some categories). The current text was revised by T.D. TTB-199 in December 2024.
- **Bottled-in-bond** labels (27 CFR 5.88) must give the real name of the distillery, or the trade name under which it distilled, and a number identifying it.
- **The COLA registry.** TTB publishes approved labels, front and back, in its public online registry.

### Go to the source

- › _27 CFR 5.66 and 5.88, at law.cornell.edu or ecfr.gov._
- › _TTB, distilled spirits label checklist, ttb.gov/media/66695/download._
- › _TTB COLAs Online: search any brand for its approved labels._

> **TRAPS**
> The labelling rules were rewritten and renumbered in late 2024; older articles cite 5.36 and 5.22. Say which version you are quoting.

## Module 7. The house bottle, and what came next

_What does the distillery do under its own name, and where is it going?_

### What's established

- **9 May 2018.** MGP announces Rossville Union, its first rye under its own label, rolling out from June 2018 in the Midwest and Southwest. The Straight Rye: a blend of the plant's 95% and 51% rye recipes, at least four years old, 94 proof, $39.99. A Barrel Proof at 112.6 proof, $69.99.
- **2021.** MGP and its Luxco brands restore the name **Ross & Squibb Distillery** for the plant's own brands; contract distilling continues as MGP.
- **2023.** Ross & Squibb master distiller Ian Stirsman releases a Rossville Union Bottled-in-Bond rye.
- **15 October 2026.** MGP Ingredients announces it will rename itself **Sempreon**, effective 2 November 2026, with the Nasdaq ticker CASK (SEC Form 8-K).
- Templeton (Iowa, 2018) and High West (Utah, distilling since 2015 at Wanship) now make some of their own rye; Bulleit makes bourbon in Kentucky and keeps its rye from Indiana.

### Go to the source

- › _Fred Minnick, 9 May 2018, and Distillery Trail, on the Rossville Union launch._
- › _BevNET, 2023, "Ross & Squibb Distillery master distiller Ian Stirsman releases 2023 Rossville Union Bottled-in-Bond"._
- › _MGP Ingredients, Form 8-K, 15 October 2026 (sec.gov)._
- › _The Whiskey Wash, "Distilling Giant MGP Ingredients Rebrands as Sempreon"._

> **TRAPS**
> Rossville Union is not only the 95% rye: it is a blend with the 51% recipe. And "Sempreon" is the company; the distillery is still Ross & Squibb, and many people will go on calling it MGP.

## Claims that don't hold up

- **"All MGP rye is the same whiskey."** Same recipe and plant; different barrels, ages, proofs, filtering and blending.
- **"Buying whiskey is cheating."** Non-distiller producers are legal, common and old; selection and blending are skills. The interesting question is what each brand added.
- **"Templeton was fined."** It settled a civil class action.

## Not confirmed

Said aloud as "the story goes" or hedged in the script, or kept off the slides.

- **The Templeton Prohibition legend** (Iowa bootleg rye with a Chicago following). No primary source found; the script says "the story goes".
- **Who George Ross was** beyond his name, date and trade.
- **When Ebersold settled on 95/5**: after 1983, or the early 1990s. The script gives no year.
- **Where Dickel Rye is charcoal-filtered**: Tennessee or Plainfield, Illinois. The script says it is unsettled.
- **Exact back-label wording** for Bulleit 95 Rye and George Dickel Rye. The law requires a state-of-distillation statement; the wording was not seen.
- **The Templeton settlement total** ($2.5 million is the reported figure).
- **Current availability** of Smooth Ambler Old Scout Rye and of Rossville Union Straight Rye in any given state.
- **Angel's Envy Rye and Filibuster** as current Lawrenceburg rye bottlings: from Wikipedia's list only.
- **Tasting cues on the slides** ("Look for…") are common tasters' impressions of these styles, offered as prompts, not sourced facts.
- **Prices** are approximate US shelf prices checked in November 2026 from retailer listings and press releases.
