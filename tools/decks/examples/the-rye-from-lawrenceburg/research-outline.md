# The Rye From Lawrenceburg

Research outline: one Indiana distillery and the American rye revival

_This is the other half of the speaker notes. It does not tell you what to say; it tells you where to look, what is settled, what is still moving, and which widely repeated claims will trip you up. Facts were checked in November 2026 against the documents listed at the end, each marked with how much of it was read. Which brands buy from which distillery, and what the distillery calls itself, change quickly: re-check anything about a current bottle against its label._

> **THE SPINE OF THE STORY**
> George Ross and two partners build the Rossville distillery at Lawrenceburg, Indiana (1847) → N. J. Walsh rebuilds it into one of the biggest in the country (from 1877); the Squibb brothers open a second distillery nearby (1869) → Prohibition: Rossville makes industrial alcohol, and the bootlegger George Remus takes control of the Squibb plant until it is padlocked (1923) → the industrial-alcohol company renames itself Rossville Union Distilleries (August 1933) and sells the plant to Seagram the day after repeal (December 1933) → for nearly seventy years it makes whiskey for Seagram's blends, and a 95% rye is worked out for them → Pernod Ricard plans to close it (2006) → sold to CL Financial of Trinidad and Tobago, as Lawrenceburg Distillers Indiana (2007) → bought by MGP Ingredients (December 2011), which sells its whiskey to other brands → the rye revival → the plant's own Rossville Union rye (2018), the Ross & Squibb name (2021–2026), then MGP Distillery (2026), and the owner renames itself Sempreon (2 November 2026).

## How to use this

Each module gives you the question worth answering, the facts that are nailed down (with dates, so you can check them), the sources, threads worth pulling, and the traps. The old history comes from an 1885 county history, Indiana and Washington newspapers of the 1920s and 1930s, and trade journals, all free online. The modern history comes from SEC filings, press releases and the trade press. Where a claim rests on one secondary source, or on a source that could not be read, it is in **Not confirmed** at the end and said aloud as "the story goes" or "one account says".

## Module 1. George Ross and the Rossville distillery

_Who built the first distillery on this ground, and what kind of town was it?_

### What's established

- **1845.** George Ross starts Lawrenceburg's first brewery, in the building known as the Old Cotton Mill (1885 county history, p. 288).
- **1847.** "The old Rossville distillery was built in 1847 by George Ross, Gid Renner and Antony Swartz, and they ran it till Ross' death" (1885 county history, p. 287). The same book, three pages earlier, says "In 1847 or 1848" and spells the partner Gid Benner. It gives the capacity as 600 bushels a day. Rossville was a settlement next to Lawrenceburg, later annexed to the city.
- **1847.** A George Ross is one of the five trustees who sign the constitution of Lawrenceburg's German Evangelical Reformed Church on 3 October 1847, and helped J. D. Hauck build its first church. The book does not say whether he is the distiller; it is likely but not shown.
- **After Ross.** The distillery passes through several hands: Rittenhouse & Shroyer; E. G. Hayes and William Probasco, who ran it during the Civil War, "when the tax was put on and made them rich"; E. B. Bradford around 1868; Smith Fowler; Alf Phillips. **1877:** N. J. Walsh buys it and rebuilds it entirely in brick, to mash 2,100 bushels a day, with pens for 1,500 cattle and warehouse room for 25,000 barrels (Registered Distillery No. 7).
- **The town.** In 1885 Dearborn County had seven distilleries, four of them at Lawrenceburg, and nearly all of a federal revenue district's $3.26 million in tax for 1879–80 was collected in the county. A revenue man is quoted: the distilleries "have made enough whisky to float a navy or flood a city."
- **The work.** Distilleries here were also feedlots: the spent grain ("slop") fattened cattle and hogs in pens beside the stills. A Rossville bookkeeper of the 1860s had "an interest in the cattle fed at the establishment" and then bought grain for the distillery.
- **1897.** "Rossville distillery, in Newtown, the largest in the state, burned today. It was worth $200,000" (Portsmouth Daily Times, 21 October 1897, a telegraph item from Lawrenceburg).

### Go to the source

- › _History of Dearborn and Ohio Counties, Indiana (F. E. Weakley & Co., Chicago, 1885), pp. 283–288, "The Manufacture of Distilled Liquors": every distillery in the city, who built it and who ran it. Free on the Internet Archive (historyofdearbor00chic). Read in part: the city chapter, pp. 270–290, and the biographies that mention Ross, Squibb and the Rossville distillery._
- › _Portsmouth Daily Times, 21 October 1897, on the Internet Archive: the fire._

### Threads worth pulling

- When did George Ross die, and who was he before 1845? The county's probate abstracts (Internet Archive, "Dearborn County, Indiana probate order book abstracts") and Lawrenceburg newspapers of the 1850s are the place to look.
- Who worked the Rossville distillery? The census of 1850–1880 lists occupations by household; the revenue service kept storekeepers and gaugers at each plant (one is named in the Seymour Daily Republican of 6 June 1902).
- When does rye become Lawrenceburg's speciality? The 1885 book describes other Lawrenceburg distilleries as making Bourbon whiskies and high wines, and does not say what Rossville made.

> **TRAPS**
> The 1847 distillery was "the Rossville distillery". "Rossville Union" is a company name from 1933 (Module 3), not Ross's. Don't call him the sole founder: the county history names two partners. And don't say the 1847 distillery was a rye distillery: the company says so, the 1885 history does not. A 1936 Commercial Solvents advertisement names the founder "James Ross", son of a Scot who had lived on Washington's estate; the 1885 history, the earlier and better source, says George.

## Module 2. Squibb, and a whiskey town

_Where does the "Squibb" in Ross & Squibb come from?_

### What's established

- **1868–1869.** Kosmos Frederick starts building a distillery in Greendale, beside Lawrenceburg, and before it is finished takes William P. and George W. Squibb as partners; it opens in January 1869. Frederick sells out to the brothers on 1 September 1871 (1885 county history, p. 286; Registered Distillery No. 8).
- **The Squibbs.** William Pinckney Squibb was born on Laughery Creek in 1831. His father was killed by an explosion during the celebration of the first train to reach Aurora; W. P. started a wholesale liquor and rectifying business at seventeen. George W. died in February 1913 and William in October 1913; their sons ran W. P. Squibb & Company as a corporation and built a new concrete and brick distillery in 1915 (1915 county history).
- **1921.** The company's own timeline says George Remus bought the Squibb distillery in 1921. A 1926 newspaper confirms the core: Remus "held the controlling interest in the distillery a few years ago" (Module 3).
- **1937.** The company's timeline says the former Squibb plant passed to the Old Quaker Company. Later owners of the Lawrenceburg plant bought the other old distillery sites, Squibb's among them (company claim).

### Go to the source

- › _History of Dearborn and Ohio Counties (1885), p. 286: the Squibb distillery. Read in part._
- › _Archibald Shaw (ed.), History of Dearborn County, Indiana (B. F. Bowen, 1915), biographies of William P., Nathaniel E. and George L. P. Squibb. Internet Archive, historyofdearbor02shaw. Read in part: those biographies._
- › _MGP Distillery, "About" timeline, mgpdistillery.com/about (read November 2026): the company's version, 1847 to 2011._

> **TRAPS**
> Rossville and Squibb were two different distilleries, run by different families, a short way apart. The present plant stands on the Rossville site (the company says the Squibb site was acquired later). Don't merge them into one founding story.

## Module 3. Prohibition: industrial alcohol, a bootlegger and a new name

_How did the distillery survive Prohibition, and where does "Rossville Union" come from?_

### What's established

- **1919.** The company's timeline says the Rossville distillery began making industrial alcohol. A trade journal of January 1929 confirms that the Rossville Company made grain alcohol at Lawrenceburg through the 1920s, and that "the Walsh interests were sold" in 1919 (the scan is damaged around that sentence). In 1924 the Rossville Company built a molasses-alcohol plant near New Orleans. In late 1928 it merged with other alcohol makers as the Rossville Commercial Alcohol Corporation.
- **1923–1926, the Squibb plant.** In December 1923 the Lawrenceburg distillery and other distilleries "operated by Remus" were padlocked after Prohibition Director Bert Morgan exposed the "Death Valley" cache. About 1,500 cases were seized from "the Squibb distillery, Lawrenceburg" after George Remus's conviction and stored in the Federal Building in Indianapolis. On 24 March 1926 Representative La Guardia told the House that 350 cases had "mysteriously disappeared": "Whisky does not walk off by itself." The whiskey was described as "110-proof whisky, reputed as being the best in the country" (Indianapolis Times, 24 March 1926, p. 1).
- **1930.** American Solvents & Chemicals Co. "plans expansion of recently acquired Rossville Distillery and Greendale Co." (Chemical Markets, August 1930).
- **28 August 1933.** "Coincident with its entry into the manufacture of whisky", the Rossville Alcohol & Chemical Co. changes its name to **Rossville Union Distilleries, Inc.** (Evening Star, Washington, 28 August 1933). That is the first use of the "Rossville Union" name found.
- **6 December 1933,** the day after repeal took effect. Distillers Corporation-Seagrams Ltd of Montreal announces it has bought the Lawrenceburg plant from Rossville Union Distilleries for $2,400,000 and 172,623 Seagram shares, "one of the largest in North America". The deal "marks the entrance of the Canadian company into the American field", and the plant is already distilling under a government permit (Indianapolis Times, 6 December 1933, p. 1; Evening Star, 6 December 1933, p. 13, which describes the cash and shares slightly differently).

### Go to the source

- › _Indianapolis Times, 24 March 1926, p. 1, "Remus Booze Missing Here, Charge". Library of Congress, Chronicling America. Read in full (the article)._
- › _Evening Star (Washington), 28 August 1933, p. 14, "Firm Changes Name". Chronicling America. Read in full._
- › _Indianapolis Times, 6 December 1933, p. 1, "State Distillery Sold to Canadian Company"; Evening Star, 6 December 1933, p. 13, corporation news. Chronicling America. Both read in full (the items)._
- › _The American Perfumer and Essential Oil Review, January 1929, on the Rossville Commercial Alcohol Corporation, with an interview with its president, Victor M. O'Shaughnessy. Internet Archive (sim_cosmetics-toiletries_1929-01_23_11). Read in part; the OCR is broken across columns._
- › _Chemical Markets, August 1930, "New Plant Construction". Internet Archive. Read the item._

### Threads worth pulling

- Why "Union"? Nothing found explains it. The 1930 item pairs "Rossville Distillery and Greendale Co."; a union of the two is a guess, not a finding.
- Fred C. Kelly, One Thing Leads to Another: The Growth of an Industry (Houghton Mifflin, 1936), is a company history that, by its search snippet, covers George Ross and the Rossville distillery. It is a lending-only scan on the Internet Archive and was not read. See Worth getting.
- Remus's Lawrenceburg years are told in books about him; the 1920s Indianapolis Times on Chronicling America has dozens of reports on the trials.

> **TRAPS**
> "One of America's last Prohibition-era distilleries" and "continuous production since 1847" are marketing lines. What the documents show is a plant that made industrial alcohol under Prohibition, changed owners several times, and only went back to whisky in 1933. Remus controlled the Squibb plant, not Rossville: say so if you tell his story.

## Module 4. Seagram and the 95% rye

_Why did one plant make such an unusual rye, and who designed it?_

### What's established

- **1933–2001.** Seagram owns the plant. Its whiskey went largely into blends such as Seagram's 5 Crown and 7 Crown; it also made Seagram's gin and vodka. Seagram's other American plant for blending stock was at Lawrenceburg, Kentucky, now Four Roses.
- **Larry Ebersold.** Born 1950; a master's in chemical engineering from the University of Louisville; started at the Lawrenceburg plant in 1972 and was master distiller there for more than twenty years; retired in 2010; inducted into the Kentucky Bourbon Hall of Fame in 2025 (Whiskey University profile; VinePair, 2025).
- **The recipe.** Two accounts, both credited to Ebersold, differ in the steps. Whiskey University and VinePair: in the early 1990s he was asked for a rye bold enough to show through in a blend; he first tried 100% malted rye, which he thinks was among the best whiskey he made, but it cost too much, and he settled on 95% rye and 5% malted barley. Chuck Cowdery (2012): they started from a 51% rye, tried 80% rye with 20% malted rye, then 95% rye with 5% malted rye, and finally replaced the rye malt with cheaper barley malt; each step was pushed by the accountants. Both agree on the end point and the reason: flavour for blends at a price.
- **Gimli.** Cowdery reports that Seagram tried to make the same rye at its Gimli, Manitoba plant for Canadian whisky and failed, because "a crucial strain of bacteria, native to Indiana, couldn't survive". One account only.
- **Greg Metze**, Ebersold's protégé, ran the distillery for 38 years and left in 2016; Ian Stirsman has been master distiller since 2021 (Whisky Advocate, 2026).
- The owner's product page today lists a 95% rye whiskey: rye 95%, barley malt 5% (sempreon.com, November 2026).

### Go to the source

- › _Aaron Goldfarb, "How the '95/5' Rye Mash Bill Went From MGP Signature to Craft Staple", VinePair, 9 October 2025. Read in full._
- › _Whiskey University, "Larry Ebersold". Read in full._
- › _Chuck Cowdery, "George Dickel Gives A Different Taste To LDI Rye", 26 October 2012, chuckcowdery.blogspot.com. Read in full: the recipe's history, Gimli, and the Dickel process._
- › _WhiskyCast, "The Godfather of Rye Whiskey", episode 730, 7 October 2018: a long interview with Ebersold. Not read (the Medium page refused)._

> **TRAPS**
> Don't give a single year for the 95/5 recipe: "the early 1990s" is the best supported. Don't confuse Lawrenceburg, Indiana with Lawrenceburg, Kentucky (Four Roses, Wild Turkey). A 2007 business report says the Indiana plant "distills Wild Turkey whiskey"; nothing else found supports that, and it may be the same confusion.

## Module 5. Nearly closed

_How did the distillery go from Seagram workhorse to contract supplier, and what happened to the people who worked there?_

### What's established

- **2001.** Pernod Ricard USA acquires the Seagram plant and brands when Seagram's drinks business is broken up (IBJ, 16 February 2007).
- **Early 2006.** Pernod says the distillery will close in phases over two years, "putting about 400 workers out of jobs" (IBJ, 16 February 2007). The exact date of the announcement was not found in a document.
- **February 2007.** Pernod agrees to sell the distillery and bottling plant to CL Financial Group of Trinidad and Tobago, a sale "hailed as saving the plant from closure". **April 2007:** Pernod notifies the state it will lay off 405 workers by 26 June (IBJ, 27 April 2007). Angostura, CL Financial's spirits company, later said its intervention had saved about 150 jobs (drinks international, 27 August 2008).
- **Lawrenceburg Distillers Indiana, LLC** is the name of the business MGP bought from, with Angostura US Holdings as its parent (asset purchase agreement, 2011). CL Financial collapsed in the 2008–09 financial crisis (from the first round of research; not re-read).
- **20 October 2011.** MGP Ingredients' subsidiary signs the agreement; **27 December 2011** it acquires the distillery's assets in Lawrenceburg and Greendale, paying cash estimated at $11 million (MGP 10-K for 2011; press releases of 21 October and 28 December 2011).
- **2012.** The plant's workers sign a multi-year contract with Local 13D of the United Food and Commercial Workers, covering 40 employees; the local radio station notes the plant had been unionised "for much of its modern history" (Eagle Country, 15 March 2012).

### Go to the source

- › _Indianapolis Business Journal, "Lawrenceburg bottling plant sold, saved from closure", 16 February 2007, and "Pernod to lay off 405 in Lawrenceburg distillery", 27 April 2007. Both read in full._
- › _drinks international, "New era for Angostura in wake of US distillery acquisition", 27 August 2008. Read in full._
- › _MGP Ingredients, Form 8-K, 21 October 2011 (press release) and 28 December 2011 (asset purchase agreement, Exhibit 2.1, and press release); Form 10-K filed 13 March 2012. sec.gov. Releases read in full; the agreement and 10-K read in part._
- › _Eagle Country 99.3, "L'burg Distillery Signs Contract With Union", 15 March 2012. Read in full._

### Threads worth pulling

- 400 jobs planned to go, 405 layoff notices, 150 saved, 40 union members in 2012: the plant's workforce shrank to a fraction. Local coverage in the Dearborn County Register and the Cincinnati Enquirer (not searched here) would tell what that meant in the town.

> **TRAPS**
> "LDI" and "MGP" whiskey are the same plant at different dates. And don't say "Pernod renamed it LDI": the LDI name belongs to the CL Financial years. The widely repeated date "19 April 2006" for the closure announcement appears without a citation; the documents found say only "early 2006".

## Module 6. The revival brands

_What did each brand do with the same rye?_

### What's established

- **Templeton was first.** Cowdery (2012): Templeton "was the first to bring LDI's unique 95% rye to market"; since then the rye had appeared from High West, Redemption, Filibuster, Smooth Ambler, James E. Pepper, Bulleit and George Dickel. Diageo, which owns Bulleit and Dickel, was the plant's biggest customer.
- **Bulleit 95 Rye.** Launched 2011; 95% rye, 5% malted barley from the Indiana plant, "as all Bulleit ryes have been, and will be"; Bulleit's two Kentucky distilleries make bourbon, not rye (Whisky Advocate, 9 July 2026). 90 proof; no age statement.
- **George Dickel Rye.** 90 proof; the same 95/5 rye. In 2012 Dickel's master distiller John Lunn said the aged whiskey was trucked from Indiana to Diageo's bottling plant at Plainfield, Illinois, where it was chilled to 40°F and filtered through sugar-maple charcoal sent from the Dickel distillery near Tullahoma, Tennessee: the same process as Dickel's Tennessee whisky, but after ageing rather than before (Cowdery, 2012). Retailer pages in 2026 variously say Tennessee or Plainfield; nothing newer than 2012 from the company was found.
- **Redemption, Smooth Ambler Old Scout, High West, Filibuster, James E. Pepper** are named in Cowdery's 2012 list. Angel's Envy Rye is named only in Wikipedia's list.

### Go to the source

- › _Julia Higgins, "Bulleit Celebrates Rye With a 20 Year Old Release", Whisky Advocate, 9 July 2026. Read in full._
- › _Chuck Cowdery, "George Dickel Gives A Different Taste To LDI Rye", 26 October 2012. Read in full._
- › _Whisky Advocate, "High West Is Making a Big Change to Double Rye"; Difford's Guide on Smooth Ambler Old Scout; Paste (April 2015) and Beverage Dynamics (March 2019) on Redemption. Not re-read in this pass; seen in the first round of research._

### Threads worth pulling

- Taste two of these blind against each other. Same recipe, same still: what is left to tell them apart is what each brand chose.
- Is Dickel Rye still filtered at Plainfield? A current back label (bottler's address) would answer it.

> **TRAPS**
> These are the same recipe from the same distillery, not the same whiskey: barrels, age, proof, filtering and blending differ. Brand lists go out of date fast; check a current bottle before you call it Lawrenceburg rye.

## Module 7. Templeton

_A town's legend, a label, and a distillery of its own._

### What's established

- **The brand.** Introduced legally in 2006 (Beverage Dynamics, 2018); Cowdery says it "debuted in 2005". Founder Keith Kerkhoff. Marketed on the town's Prohibition reputation for illegal whiskey.
- **The whiskey.** Distilled and aged in Indiana, finished and bottled in Iowa (Beverage Dynamics, 2018). Kerkhoff acknowledged the Indiana source in a 2010 Chicago Tribune article.
- **The settlement (2015).** A Chicago man, Christopher McNair, sued in Cook County in 2014; a second suit followed there and a third in Polk County, Iowa. Under the preliminary settlement announced 14 July 2015, anyone who bought Templeton Rye since 2006 could claim $6 a bottle with proof of purchase or $3 without, up to six bottles. The back label would say "Distilled in Indiana", and "Small Batch" and "Prohibition Era Recipe" would come off the front (Chicago Tribune, 14 July 2015).
- **The distillery.** Work began in April 2017; the 34,500-square-foot, $35 million distillery opened in Templeton in August 2018 (Beverage Dynamics, 7 August 2018).
- **Templeton Straight Rye (November 2024).** 95% rye, 5% malted barley, 90 proof, "selected from sourced rye whiskey inventory", aged at least four years, bottled at Templeton, $29.99 suggested (Fred Minnick, 8 November 2024). The release does not say where the whiskey was distilled.
- **30 October 2025.** Templeton's first rye distilled, aged and bottled at its own distillery: a limited single barrel, 95/5, barrel proof, $49.99 (Infinium Spirits press release).

## Module 8. Reading the back label

_What does federal law require a whiskey label to say about where it was distilled?_

### What's established

- **27 CFR 5.66(f)(1), state of distillation for whisky.** "The State of distillation, which is the State in which original distillation takes place, must appear on the label of any type of whisky defined in § 5.143(c)(2) through (7), (15), and (16), which is distilled in the United States." It may be shown in one of four ways: a "distilled by" statement with a single location; a "bottled by" address that is in the state of distillation; the state next to the type ("Kentucky bourbon whisky"), only if distilled and aged there; or a separate statement "such as 'Distilled in [name of State].'" Types (2) to (7) include bourbon, rye, wheat, malt and corn whisky.
- **5.66(f)(2).** TTB may require the state of distillation even where it already appears in the name and address, "to negate any misleading or deceptive impression".
- **History of the text.** The rule was in 27 CFR 5.36 before 2022. The current numbering and wording come from TTB's labelling modernisation rule (published 9 February 2022, effective 11 March 2022). The American single malt rule (published 18 December 2024, effective 19 January 2025) added types (15) and (16) to the list.
- **27 CFR 5.88(c), bottled in bond.** A domestic bonded whisky "must bear the real name of the distillery or the trade name under which the distiller distilled and warehoused the spirits, and the number of the distilled spirits plant in which distilled, and the number of the distilled spirits plant in which bottled."
- **The COLA registry.** TTB publishes approved labels in its public online registry, COLAs Online.

### Go to the source

- › _eCFR, 27 CFR 5.66 and 5.88, ecfr.gov (text current to 19 November 2026), and the version history of 5.66. Read in full (those sections)._
- › _Federal Register, "Modernization of the Labeling and Advertising Regulations for Distilled Spirits and Malt Beverages", 9 February 2022 (FR Doc. 2022-00841); "Addition of American Single Malt Whisky to the Standards of Identity for Distilled Spirits", 18 December 2024 (FR Doc. 2024-29938). Titles and dates read; rule texts not read._
- › _TTB COLAs Online: search any brand for its approved labels. Could not be reached from the research environment._

> **TRAPS**
> The rule is not "if the address is in another state". Most American whisky must show its state of distillation one way or another; a "Distilled in Indiana" line is one of the four ways. Older articles cite 5.36; say which version you quote.

## Module 9. The house bottle, and the names since

_What does the distillery sell under its own name, and what is it called now?_

### What's established

- **9 May 2018.** MGP announces Rossville Union, its first proprietary rye, "named after the founding distillery", with "1847" embossed on the flask. Master Crafted Straight Rye, at least four years old, 94 proof, $39.99, from 159 barrels; Barrel Proof, 112.6 proof, $69.99, from 83 barrels; in twelve Midwest and Mountain states from June 2018 (PR Newswire).
- **The recipe.** MGP's own FAQ, quoted by Bourbon & Banter in June 2018: "We have focused this bottling on a combination of our 51% and 95% rye mash bills", aged five to six years.
- **29 September 2021.** Luxco, which MGP bought that year, names the plant **Ross & Squibb Distillery**, after Ross's Rossville distillery, "located on what is now the Ross & Squibb Distillery campus", and the Squibb distillery Remus bought. Contract work continues as MGPI of Indiana, LLC.
- **September 2026.** With its first MGP-branded bourbon, MGP drops the Ross & Squibb name and calls the plant **MGP Distillery** (Drinks Intel, 9 September 2026; Whisky Advocate, 2026). rossandsquibb.com now redirects to mgpdistillery.com.
- **14–15 October 2026.** MGP Ingredients files a Form 8-K: on 14 October it amended its articles to change its name to **Sempreon, Inc.**, effective 2 November 2026, with the Nasdaq ticker changing from MGPI to CASK. The attached release calls the Lawrenceburg plant "now known as MGP Distillery". EDGAR now lists the company as Sempreon, Inc.
- **November 2026.** The Rossville Union site still lists the Master Crafted rye at 94 proof; the MGP Distillery brands page lists MGP, Remus and Penelope, not Rossville Union.
- Templeton (Iowa, since 2018) and High West (Utah) now make some of their own rye; Bulleit makes bourbon in Kentucky and keeps its rye from Indiana.

## Claims that don't hold up

- **"Rossville Union Distillery, founded 1847."** Ross's distillery was the Rossville distillery; Rossville Union Distilleries, Inc. is the 1933 name of the alcohol company that sold it to Seagram.
- **"In continuous production since 1847."** It made industrial alcohol under Prohibition and changed hands many times.
- **"George Remus owned this distillery."** He controlled the Squibb distillery nearby, later absorbed by the plant's owners.
- **"All MGP rye is the same whiskey."** Same recipe and plant; different barrels, ages, proofs, filtering and blending.
- **"Buying whiskey is cheating."** Non-distiller producers are legal, common and old; selection and blending are skills.
- **"Templeton was fined."** It settled a civil class action.
- **"The plant is still Ross & Squibb."** Not since September 2026.

## Not confirmed

Said aloud as "the story goes" or hedged in the script, or kept off the slides.

- **That Ross's distillery made rye.** The company says so; the 1885 history does not say what Rossville made (it describes its neighbours as making Bourbon whiskies and high wines). Not shown.
- **George Ross's life** beyond the brewery (1845), the distillery (1847) and that he ran it till his death; his death date was not found. Whether the church trustee of 1847 is the same man.
- **The exact dates of Remus's purchase (1921) and of the Squibb plant's sale to Old Quaker (1937).** Company timeline only; the 1926 newspaper confirms Remus's control "a few years ago".
- **The 1919 sale of the Walsh interests**, from a damaged scan.
- **The date of Pernod's closure announcement.** "Early 2006" is documented; "19 April 2006" is not.
- **Which recipe sequence is right** (100% malted rye first, or 51% → 80/20 → 95/5): both are credited to Ebersold. The script gives neither.
- **The Gimli story** (Cowdery, 2012, one account).
- **Where Dickel Rye is filtered now.** Plainfield, Illinois, per Dickel's master distiller in 2012; nothing newer from the company.
- **Where Templeton Straight Rye was distilled.** The 2024 release says only "sourced"; the slide's "Distilled in Lawrenceburg, for this bottle" needs a look at the back label.
- **Bulleit 95 Rye's bottling location** and the exact back-label wording for Bulleit and Dickel. COLAs Online could not be reached.
- **The Templeton settlement total** (about $2.5 million is the reported figure; not found in the articles read).
- **Angel's Envy Rye** as a Lawrenceburg rye: Wikipedia's list only. **Filibuster**, **Redemption**, **Smooth Ambler**, **High West**: Cowdery's 2012 list; current sourcing not re-checked.
- **That Rossville Union is still sold**: its own site is up; the distillery's brands page omits it.
- **That the Seagram name is still on the Ridge Avenue brickwork** (the photograph is undated).
- **Tasting cues on the slides** ("Look for…") are common tasters' impressions of these styles, offered as prompts, not sourced facts.
