# MGP Rye

Speaker's notes

The Whiskey Share Society

## Part One: before the night

### The short version

A distillery in Lawrenceburg, Indiana made a 95% rye for Seagram's blends. It nearly closed in 2006. Its later owners sold that rye to other brands, and a lot of the American rye sold since 2011 was distilled there. Tonight we pour four of those ryes.

### What the night is

Pour glass 1 first and tell the distillery's history while people drink it. Then go one bottle at a time: Bulleit, George Dickel, Templeton, and the distillery's own Rossville Union.

Be fair to the brands. Each of them chose the barrels, the age and the proof. Some of them filtered or blended the rye. Several now have distilleries of their own.

### How many bottles

Four. You can add High West Double Rye at the end to make five. More than five is too many to taste properly.

### Cast the roles, then find the bottles

- **Glass 1: the Lawrenceburg rye that is easiest to find.** Suggested bottle: Bulleit 95 Rye.
- **Glass 2: the same rye with a step added by the brand.** Suggested bottle: George Dickel Rye, which is filtered through sugar-maple charcoal.
- **Glass 3: a brand named for a town that has since built a distillery there.** Suggested bottle: Templeton Straight Rye.
- **Glass 4: a rye sold by the distillery itself.** Suggested bottle: Rossville Union Straight Rye.

### Bottle ideas by role

Prices are rough US shelf prices, checked in November 2026. They vary by state. Buy what you can find.

**Glass 1: easiest to find**

- **Bulleit 95 Rye.** About $25 to $33. 90 proof. 95% rye and 5% malted barley.
- **Redemption Rye.** About $25 to $30. 92 proof. The brand has always said its rye comes from Indiana.

**Glass 2: a step added by the brand**

- **George Dickel Rye.** About $22 to $30. 90 proof. Charcoal filtered.
- **Angel's Envy Rye.** Lawrenceburg rye that the brand finishes itself. It costs more. Check the price and the back label.
- **High West Double Rye.** 92 proof. Check the price locally. It is Indiana 95% rye blended with High West's own Utah rye. Here the step the brand adds is blending.

**Glass 3: named for a town**

- **Templeton Straight Rye.** About $30. 90 proof. The standard bottle is still made from rye Templeton buys. Its own Iowa rye has only come out once, as a limited single barrel in October 2025.
- **Smooth Ambler Old Scout Rye.** Smooth Ambler is a West Virginia company. It uses the name "Old Scout" for whiskey it buys. It can be hard to find.

**Glass 4: sold by the distillery**

- **Rossville Union Straight Rye.** About $40. 94 proof. A blend of the distillery's 95% rye and its 51% rye.
- **Rossville Union Bottled-in-Bond or Barrel Proof.** Either one works as the last glass if you can find it.

## Part Two: the script

_SET UP: four glasses per person, marked 1 to 4. Pour glass 1 before slide 2 if you can. Leave the bottles on the table._

## [SLIDE 1] MGP Rye

Good evening.

Tonight is about one distillery, in Lawrenceburg, Indiana.

For most of its life it made whiskey for Seagram's blends. Since 2011 it has sold its rye to other companies, and they bottle it under their own names. A lot of the rye on American shelves was distilled there.

We have four of those ryes tonight.

## [SLIDE 2] Glass 1: Bulleit 95 Rye

_Pour glass 1, the Bulleit 95 Rye. Keep talking while you pour._

This is Bulleit 95 Rye. Drink it whenever you like.

The 95 is the recipe. It's 95% rye and 5% malted barley. Many American ryes are only a little over half rye, and much of the rest is corn. So the spice and the grassy, herbal notes in this glass come mostly from the rye.

Keep a little in the glass. George Dickel Rye and Templeton Rye, glasses 2 and 3, are made to the same recipe, and it helps to taste them together.

## [SLIDE 3] 1847: the Rossville distillery

In 1847 George Ross and two partners built a distillery at Rossville, a settlement next to Lawrenceburg, Indiana. Ross had started the town's first brewery two years before.

A county history printed in 1885 lists the owners after Ross. It says two of them ran it during the Civil War, "when the tax was put on and made them rich."

In 1877 a man named N. J. Walsh bought it and rebuilt it in brick. It could mash 2,100 bushels of grain a day. In October 1897 it burned. A newspaper report of the fire called it the largest distillery in the state and put its value at $200,000.

Today's owner says the Rossville distillery made rye. The 1885 history doesn't say what it made.

The distillery started using the Rossville name again in 2018, for its Rossville Union rye.

## [SLIDE 4] 1885: a whiskey town

By 1885 the county had seven distilleries, and four of them were in Lawrenceburg. A revenue officer said they had made "enough whisky to float a navy or flood a city."

The distilleries were also feedlots. The grain left over after distilling was fed to cattle and hogs, which were kept in pens beside the stills. When Walsh rebuilt Rossville he put in pens for 1,500 cattle.

Two brothers called Squibb opened their own distillery nearby in 1869.

## [SLIDE 5] Prohibition

Prohibition began in 1920. The Rossville distillery made industrial alcohol until it ended.

The Squibb distillery was controlled by George Remus, the Cincinnati bootlegger. In December 1923 it was padlocked, along with the other distilleries he ran.

About 1,500 cases of whiskey were seized from the Squibb distillery and stored in the Federal Building in Indianapolis. On the 24th of March 1926 the Indianapolis Times reported on its front page that 350 of those cases were missing. Congressman Fiorello La Guardia told the House, "Whisky does not walk off by itself."

The paper said it was 110-proof whiskey, "reputed as being the best in the country."

_The distillery's owner sells a bourbon called Remus today. If someone has a bottle, bring it out now._

## [SLIDE 6] 1933: Seagram

In August 1933 Prohibition was about to end. The industrial alcohol company at Rossville changed its name to Rossville Union Distilleries and went back to making whiskey.

Prohibition ended on the 5th of December 1933. On the 6th, the Indianapolis Times reported that Seagram, the Canadian company, had bought the plant. The price was $2.4 million plus Seagram shares. The paper called it one of the largest distilleries in North America. It said this was how Seagram got into the United States.

The photograph is the Seagram's building on Ridge Avenue in Lawrenceburg.

Seagram owned the plant for nearly seventy years. Much of the whiskey it made there went into blends.

## [SLIDE 7] A rye made for blending

Seagram's business was blended whiskey. 7 Crown and 5 Crown were two of its blends. A blend gets much of its flavor from a small amount of strong-tasting whiskey.

Larry Ebersold started at the plant in 1972 and was its master distiller for more than twenty years. In the early 1990s he was asked for a rye strong enough to taste through a blend.

Ebersold says he first tried a whiskey made entirely from malted rye. He says it was among the best whiskey he ever made, and that it cost too much. He settled on 95% rye and 5% malted barley.

One account says Seagram tried to make the same rye at its plant in Manitoba and couldn't.

The rye in your glass was made to go into blends. For years it wasn't sold on its own.

## [SLIDE 8] Three owners in ten years

_Ask what people are getting from glass 1 before you start this slide._

Seagram's drinks business was broken up, and in 2001 the Lawrenceburg plant went to Pernod Ricard.

Early in 2006 Pernod said it would close the plant in stages. About 400 people worked there.

In February 2007 Pernod agreed to sell it to CL Financial, a holding company from Trinidad and Tobago. The business was called Lawrenceburg Distillers Indiana, and older reviews call its whiskey "LDI". The newspapers said the sale had saved the plant. That April Pernod still filed layoff notices for 405 workers. The new owner said later that it had saved about 150 jobs.

In December 2011 MGP Ingredients, a Kansas company, bought the distillery for about $11 million. MGP made whiskey and sold it to other companies to bottle.

## [SLIDE 9] The brands that bottled it

Rye was becoming popular again, and new brands wanted to sell it. Rye needs years in a barrel, so a new brand couldn't make its own in time. The Lawrenceburg plant had aged rye and was willing to sell it.

Templeton, in Iowa, was the first to bottle it. Bulleit Rye came out in 2011. George Dickel, the Tennessee distillery, put out a rye. So did Redemption, Smooth Ambler in West Virginia, High West in Utah, and others. The slide doesn't show all of them.

Each brand started with the same rye and chose its own age and proof. Some filtered it or blended it. Bulleit, George Dickel and Templeton are three of those brands.

## [SLIDE 10] Glass 1 again: Bulleit 95 Rye

Go back to glass 1, the Bulleit.

Bulleit Rye came out in 2011. It is 95% rye and 5% malted barley from the Indiana plant. It is bottled at 90 proof and the label gives no age.

Bulleit has two distilleries of its own in Kentucky, and they make its bourbon. All of its rye has come from Indiana, and the company says that won't change.

_Ask: "What in this glass would you call 'rye'?"_

## [SLIDE 11] Glass 2: George Dickel Rye

_Pour glass 2._

George Dickel is a Tennessee distillery. Its rye is the same 95% recipe as glass 1, and it is distilled in Lawrenceburg.

The difference is charcoal. Dickel filters its Tennessee whisky through sugar-maple charcoal before it goes into the barrel. The rye is filtered the same way after it comes out of the barrel. In 2012 Dickel's master distiller said the aged rye was trucked to a bottling plant in Plainfield, Illinois. There it was chilled to 40 degrees and filtered through charcoal sent up from the Dickel distillery.

That's what the company said in 2012. It may have changed since.

It is usually the cheapest of tonight's four, at under $30.

_If you played the optional game, give the answer now. Then ask: "Taste glasses 1 and 2 together. Can you taste the charcoal filtering?"_

## [SLIDE 12] Glass 3: Templeton

_Pour glass 3._

Templeton is a town in Iowa. The brand came out in the mid-2000s. It says the town made bootleg rye during Prohibition, and it sold the whiskey on that story.

The whiskey was distilled and aged in Indiana and bottled in Iowa. The label said little about Indiana. A Chicago man sued in 2014, and two more lawsuits followed. They were settled in July 2015. Buyers could claim $3 a bottle, or $6 with a receipt, for up to six bottles. The company agreed to put "Distilled in Indiana" on the back label and to take "Small Batch" and "Prohibition Era Recipe" off the front.

In 2018 Templeton opened its own distillery in the town. It cost $35 million. That whiskey needed time to age.

The Straight Rye in your glass came out in November 2024. It is at least four years old and 90 proof, and it uses the 95% recipe. The company says it is selected from whiskey it bought. The company didn't say where it was distilled. The back label should name the state.

In October 2025 Templeton released its first rye distilled, aged and bottled in Iowa. It was a limited single barrel.

_The photograph on the slide is the Templeton 6 Year, not the Straight Rye. Say so if anyone asks._

_Ask: "Of the three so far, which would you buy again?"_

## [SLIDE 13] Reading the back label

_Pick up any bottle on the table and turn it round._

Federal labeling rules say most American whiskey has to show the state where it was distilled. "Distilled in Indiana" is one way a label can do that. It is usually on the back, in small type.

Every approved label is also in a public database run by the government, called the COLA registry. You can look up any bottle.

_Give people a minute to find the line on tonight's bottles._

## [SLIDE 14] Glass 4: Rossville Union

_Pour glass 4._

In May 2018 MGP released its first rye under its own label and called it Rossville Union. Rossville was the 1847 distillery. Rossville Union was the name the company took in 1933.

This is a blend of two of the distillery's ryes. One is the 95% rye from the first three glasses. The other is a 51% rye. It is at least four years old, 94 proof, and about $40.

The plant has had two more names since. In 2021 it became Ross & Squibb Distillery, after the two families we heard about earlier. In September this year it was renamed MGP Distillery.

This is the only bottle tonight that is sold by the distillery that made it.

_Ask: "What does the 51% rye change?"_

## [SLIDE 15] Where the brands are now

Some of the brands that bought Lawrenceburg rye now make their own as well.

Templeton has its distillery in Iowa. High West blends its own Utah rye with rye from Indiana. Bulleit makes bourbon in Kentucky and still buys its rye from Indiana.

The owner has changed its name too. On the 2nd of November 2026, MGP Ingredients became Sempreon.

## [SLIDE 16] Sláinte

Raise a glass to George Ross and his partners, to Larry Ebersold, and to the people making rye in Lawrenceburg now. Sláinte.
