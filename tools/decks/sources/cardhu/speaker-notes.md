## Part One: before the night

### The one-sentence version

Cardhu is a Speyside distillery that began on a farm in 1811. Two women in the Cumming family, Helen and her daughter-in-law Elizabeth, are the best-known people in its history. The family sold it to John Walker & Sons in 1893. In 2003 its owner put malts from other distilleries in the Cardhu bottle, and other distillers protested.

### What the night is

Four whiskies, poured in the order of the story. The story starts on a farm in 1811 and ends with new labelling rules in 2009. This is not a blind tasting. The bottles are on the table from the start.

Diageo and William Grant & Sons were on opposite sides in 2003. The script gives what each of them did and does not take a side.

### How many bottles

Four. Three are 40 percent and one is 43 percent.

### Cast the roles, then find the bottles

- **Glass 1: Cardhu's own single malt.** Suggested bottle: Cardhu 12 Year Old.
- **Glass 2: the distillery that started with Cardhu's old stills.** Suggested bottle: Glenfiddich 12 Year Old.
- **Glass 3: a Johnnie Walker blend.** John Walker & Sons bought the distillery in 1893. Suggested bottle: Johnnie Walker Black Label 12 Year Old.
- **Glass 4: a blended malt.** This is malt whisky from several distilleries, with no grain whisky. Cardhu Pure Malt was this kind of whisky. Suggested bottle: Johnnie Walker Green Label 15 Year Old.

## Part Two: the script

_SET UP: glass 1, Cardhu 12, poured or ready to pour. The other three bottles on the table. Water out._

## [SLIDE 1] Cardhu: The Women Behind Johnnie Walker's Malt

Welcome, and thanks for coming.

Tonight is about one Speyside distillery. It is called Cardhu now. For most of its history it was called Cardow. The two best-known people in its story are Helen Cumming and her daughter-in-law, Elizabeth Cumming. In 1893 the family sold it to John Walker & Sons, the company that makes Johnnie Walker. It was the first distillery that company owned.

We have four whiskies tonight.

## [SLIDE 2] Glass 1: Cardhu 12 Year Old

_Pour glass 1 now if it is not already poured. Keep talking while you pour._

This is Cardhu 12. It is a single malt, so all of it was made at Cardhu. It is bottled at 40 percent.

Diageo owns Cardhu and Johnnie Walker, and it says Cardhu is one of the main malts in Johnnie Walker.

_Ask: "What do you taste first? Is there any smoke in it?"_

## [SLIDE 3] John and Helen Cumming leased Cardow in 1811

In 1811 a farmer called John Cumming took a nineteen-year lease on a farm called Cardow. It is on a hill above the River Spey, in the parish of Knockando. A writer called Alfred Barnard went there in the 1880s. He called it "a wild and desolate district".

John was married to Helen. The family made whisky on the farm without a licence. One account says Helen made the whisky and was good at it. The same account says John was convicted of illegal distilling three times in 1816 and paid small fines.

The distillery counts 1811 as the year it started. It names John and Helen as its founders.

## [SLIDE 4] The stories told about Helen Cumming

The distillery tells three stories about Helen. They come from the family and the distillery, and there is no written record from the time.

In the first one, Helen hears that the excise officers are coming. They were the tax men who looked for illegal stills. She hides the still, rubs flour on her arms and tells them she has been baking.

In the second one, the officers are staying at the farm, because there was no inn nearby. While they eat the supper Helen has cooked, she goes out to the barn and puts up a red flag. The neighbours were making whisky too, and the flag told them to hide their stills.

In the third one, she walks barefoot over the hills to Elgin to deliver whisky.

The distillery flies a red flag today, next to a statue of Helen.

> **IF SOMEONE ASKS**
> None of the three stories is in a record from the time. Helen lived into her late nineties. Some accounts say 97 and some say 98.

## [SLIDE 5] Cardow was licensed in 1824

In 1823 Parliament cut the tax on whisky. The idea was to get illegal distillers to take a licence. In 1824 John Cumming took one for Cardow.

John and Helen's eldest son was called Lewis. He was 21 that year. He ran the distillery for most of the next fifty years, which is longer than anyone else in this story. He died in February 1872, aged 69.

## [SLIDE 6] Elizabeth Cumming took over in 1872

Lewis's wife was called Elizabeth. She was a local farmer's daughter and 24 years younger than him. When Lewis died she was pregnant with their fourth child. Their five-year-old daughter died three days after he did.

Elizabeth took over the farm and the distillery. In the 1870s and 1880s blended Scotch was selling more every year, and the companies that made it wanted malt whisky to put in it. Cardow's buildings were old. The farm was on a lease that ran out every nineteen years.

## [SLIDE 7] Elizabeth built a new distillery in 1884

The old distillery stood on leased land. The Cummings did not own the ground it was built on.

Alfred Barnard was going round the distilleries of Britain and Ireland for a book. He saw the old Cardow and called the buildings "straggling and primitive". He said a lot of the work was done by hand.

Elizabeth got a piece of land of her own next to the old distillery. In 1884 and 1885 she built a new distillery on it. Barnard said the old one made 25,000 gallons a year and the new one could make 60,000. In his book he does not give her first name. He calls her "Mrs. Cumming, the proprietress".

Barnard also wrote down what he was told about the whisky. It was thick and rich, and one gallon of it was enough to flavour ten gallons of plain spirit. That is what the blending companies wanted from a malt.

## [SLIDE 8] The old stills went to Glenfiddich

In January 1886 a bigger company asked whether Elizabeth would sell Cardow. She wrote back that she could not. She had three sons to provide for, and she said "it would not be justice to my family".

Later that year her eldest son died. Her son John left medical school in Aberdeen and came home to run the distillery for her.

The new distillery had new equipment, so the old equipment was for sale. A man called William Grant was building a distillery of his own at Dufftown, a few miles away. He had worked at another distillery for twenty years. In 1886 he bought the old Cardow equipment for about £120. That included a worn pair of stills.

Grant's distillery was Glenfiddich. It made its first spirit on Christmas Day 1887.

> **DO NOT OVERSELL IT**
> The price is "about £120". The stills at Glenfiddich today are not the Cardow pair, and nobody says they are copies of them.

## [SLIDE 9] Glass 2: Glenfiddich 12 Year Old

_Pour glass 2 now._

This is Glenfiddich 12, from William Grant's distillery at Dufftown. It is bottled at 40 percent. Grant's family still owns the company.

Glenfiddich and Cardhu are both Speyside single malts, and these two are both 12 years old.

_Ask: "Taste it next to glass 1. What is different?"_

## [SLIDE 10] In 1893 the Cummings sold Cardow to John Walker & Sons

In 1893 the family did sell. The buyer was John Walker & Sons, a whisky blending company in Kilmarnock. They signed on 19 September. The price was £20,500, and the whisky already in the warehouses was extra.

Elizabeth made sure her son John was part of the deal. He kept running the distillery. He got 100 shares in John Walker & Sons, worth £5,000, and a seat on its board. He stayed on the board until 1923.

Cardow was the first distillery Walker owned. Diageo says Walker bought it to be sure of getting the malt.

Elizabeth died suddenly the next May. She was 67. A local newspaper called her "a true friend of all the poor". She had lent money to neighbours without charging interest.

## [SLIDE 11] Diageo calls Cardhu a key malt in Johnnie Walker

Today Diageo owns both Cardhu and Johnnie Walker. In 2019 it called Cardhu "a key single malt in Johnnie Walker today". It named Cardhu as one of four distilleries that are homes of Johnnie Walker. The other three are Glenkinchie, Caol Ila and Clynelish.

Diageo does not publish the recipe for any of its blends. Whisky guides say Black Label has Cardhu in it, along with Caol Ila, Clynelish, Lagavulin and Talisker. Black Label also has a lot of grain whisky in it. So Cardhu is one of many whiskies in Black Label, and nobody outside the company knows how much.

## [SLIDE 12] Glass 3: Johnnie Walker Black Label

_Pour glass 3 now._

This is Johnnie Walker Black Label. It is a blend of malt whiskies and grain whiskies, all at least 12 years old. It is bottled at 40 percent.

_Ask: "Go back to glass 1. Can you taste the Cardhu in this one?"_

## [SLIDE 13] In 2003 Diageo sold Cardhu Pure Malt

In the 1990s Cardhu started selling very well in Spain and Portugal. Sales there went from 30,000 cases a year to 280,000. Diageo said the distillery could not make any more 12-year-old Cardhu than it already did, and that any new whisky would take twelve years to be ready.

In the summer of 2003 Diageo started selling a whisky called Cardhu Pure Malt. It was Cardhu mixed with malt from other Speyside distilleries. The bottle looked almost the same as before. The word "single" on the label was changed to "pure".

Other whisky companies protested. One of the loudest was William Grant & Sons, the owners of Glenfiddich. One of its senior managers wrote that the industry was "united and outraged". The row was raised in Parliament in London and in the Scottish Parliament.

On 4 December 2003 Diageo agreed to change the Pure Malt bottle from brown to green. It promised not to do the same thing to any other single malt, and it said sorry. In March 2004 it stopped making Cardhu Pure Malt and said the single malt would come back.

> **GET THE DATES RIGHT**
> A lot of summaries say Cardhu Pure Malt came out in December 2003. It went on sale in July. December is when Diageo agreed to change the bottle.

## [SLIDE 14] Since 2009 the law bans "pure malt"

New rules for Scotch whisky came into force on 23 November 2009.

A whisky made from the single malts of more than one distillery now has to be called a blended malt. Nobody can use the words "pure malt" on Scotch. And a distillery's name can only be used as a brand if all the whisky in the bottle was made at that distillery. Cardhu is on the list of distillery names the rule covers.

The rules came six years after the row over Cardhu Pure Malt. They do not mention it, and the work on the definitions had other causes as well.

## [SLIDE 15] Glass 4: Johnnie Walker Green Label

_Pour glass 4 now._

This is Johnnie Walker Green Label. It is 15 years old and bottled at 43 percent. It is a blended malt, so it is malt whisky from several distilleries with no grain whisky. Cardhu Pure Malt was the same kind of whisky.

Green Label came out in 1997 and was called Johnnie Walker Pure Malt. It got its present name in 2004. The malts usually named for it are Talisker, Linkwood, Cragganmore and Caol Ila. Cardhu is not one of them.

_Ask: "Taste glass 1 again. If this one had said Cardhu on the label, would you have noticed the difference?"_
