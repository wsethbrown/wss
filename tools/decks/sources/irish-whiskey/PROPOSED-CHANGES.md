# Irish Whiskey: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/The-Long-Way-Back/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below is
a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/The-Long-Way-Back.md`; "R B" for its blocking items, "R S" for should-fix, "R
minor" for its minor list) and my own read of the script against the slides and the outline
("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slide 1 (hook) and deck.json `description`. The market claim.**
   Current, slide: "How the biggest whiskey trade of the nineteenth century fell to two distilleries, and how it came back." deck.json: "How Irish whiskey went from the biggest whiskey trade of the 1800s to two distilleries by 1980, and how it recovered after 1987. Four pours."
   Proposed, slide: "How the best-regarded whiskey of the nineteenth century came down to two distilleries, and how it came back." deck.json: see item 30.
   Why: the outline's Module 1 trap says to find export and output figures before claiming a market share, and none are in the outline. The script already says "best-regarded" (R B2).

2. **Slide 6 (slide body and script). The Royal Commission.**
   Current, slide: "The ruling cleared the way for blended Scotch." Script: "The blenders now had the law on their side,"
   Proposed, slide: "The report cleared the way for blended Scotch." Script: "The blenders now had official backing,"
   Why: the Commission issued a report and recommended no restriction; no law was passed (R S3).

3. **Slide 11 (script). Tullamore and Kilbeggan.**
   Current: "Tullamore and Locke's at Kilbeggan, the distillery on our cover, had already stopped in 1954."
   Proposed: "Tullamore and Locke's at Kilbeggan, the distillery on our cover, had already stopped in the mid-1950s, Tullamore in 1954 by most accounts."
   Why: "Not confirmed" says Tullamore's 1954 rests on Wikipedia alone; Module 5 gives Locke's as 1954 with 1953 or 1958 from others (R S3).

4. **Slide 9 (script). Why Scotch won after Repeal.**
   Current: "The Irish distillers had less stock, fewer agents, and a style that was heavier and dearer to make."
   Proposed: "By most accounts the Irish distillers had less stock ready, fewer agents, and a style that was heavier and dearer to make." Or add a source to Module 4.
   Why: not in Module 4's "Nailed down", and its trap warns against single-cause explanations (R S3).

5. **Slide 12 (script). Coleraine.**
   Current: "Once Midleton could make grain whiskey, Coleraine was no longer needed, and it closed in 1978."
   Proposed: "Coleraine closed in 1978."
   Why: the outline gives only the closing year, not the cause (R S3).

6. **Slide 16 (script). Bought-in whiskey.**
   Current: "Look on the back label for where it was distilled, and if it only names the company and not a distillery, you can assume it was bought in."
   Proposed: "Look on the back label for where it was distilled. If it only names the company and not a distillery, it's worth asking where it was distilled; it may well have been bought in."
   Why: some distilling brands name only the company (R S3).

7. **Slide 7 (script). Belfast and Bushmills after partition.**
   Current: "and kept selling into the British market on the same terms as before."
   Proposed: "and kept selling into the British market." Or add a source to Module 4.
   Why: not in the outline (R S3).

8. **Slide 2 (script). Barnard's count.**
   Current: "twenty-nine of them in Ireland"
   Proposed: "about thirty of them in Ireland" (or "twenty-nine of them in Ireland, by one count").
   Why: Module 1 says "29 in Ireland by one count" (R S3).

9. **Slide 2 (slide and script). Where the whiskey went.**
   Current, slide: "sold it across Britain and the Empire." Script: "it went all over Britain, the colonies and America."
   Proposed: pick one wording for both, and add a source for it to Module 1 (neither is in the outline).
   Why: slide and script disagree (R minor 5); found: neither is sourced.

10. **Slide 5 (script). Coffey's still.**
    Current: "In 1830 a man called Aeneas Coffey patented a new kind of still."
    Proposed: "In 1830 a man called Aeneas Coffey patented an improved continuous still."
    Why: Module 3's trap: he improved and patented a design; others had continuous stills earlier (R minor 6).

11. **Slide 11 (slide). Bow Street's date.**
    Current: "1971 · Bow Street stops distilling."
    Proposed: keep; the script carries "(some sources say 1972)". Optionally "1971 or 1972" on the slide.
    Why: "Not confirmed" lists the disagreement (R minor 2).

12. **Slide 6 (script), found.**
    Current: "It's the British version of the American "What is whisky?" fight from the same years." and "the Scotch blends that people still know by name were already selling hard in England and abroad."
    Proposed: add sources to Module 3, or cut both clauses.
    Why: neither is in the outline.

13. **Slide 14 (script), found.**
    Current: "Teeling's sons opened a distillery in Dublin in 2015" and "new distilleries opened in most counties. By the 2020s there were dozens."
    Proposed: "The Teeling distillery opened in Dublin in 2015" (or add a source naming the sons), and "new distilleries opened across the island. By the 2020s there were dozens, by most counts." with a dated count added to Module 6.
    Why: Module 6 names the distillery, not the sons, and its trap asks for a dated source for any count. Slide 1's script ("Today there are dozens again") has the same gap.

14. **Slide 13 (script), found.**
    Current: "It's bottled at 43.2 percent, a little stronger than most standard blends."
    Proposed: "It's bottled at 43.2 percent." Or add a source for the comparison.
    Why: the comparison is not in the outline.

15. **Slide 15 (slide note and script), found.**
    Current, slide: "Peat smoke and two distillations, both unusual in Irish whiskey when Cooley started." Script: "They dried some of their malt over peat smoke, which most Irish distillers had stopped doing long before".
    Proposed: add a source to Module 6 for both claims (peating and double distillation being unusual in Ireland by 1996), or soften the script to "which few Irish distillers were doing by then".
    Why: Module 6 records Connemara as peated and double distilled but not that either was unusual.

16. **Slide 9 (script), found.**
    Current: "America had been one of the biggest markets for Irish whiskey".
    Proposed: add export figures to Module 4, or "America had been an important market for Irish whiskey".
    Why: the same market-size caution as item 1; Module 4 lists the spirits trade statistics only as primary documents to check.
