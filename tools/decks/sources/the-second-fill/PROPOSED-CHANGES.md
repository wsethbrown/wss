# The Second Fill: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/The-Second-Fill/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below
is a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/The-Second-Fill.md`, "R" plus its severity and item number) and my own read
("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## 0. The owner's decision first: does the topic stay?

1. **Whole deck (R blocking B1).** The review flags this as the owner's own example of a weak topic
   ("a presentation like 'the barrel' may not have anything specific to focus on"). About nine of
   the 17 slides are barrel basics (2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 16). The bottle chain (Maker's Mark to
   Laphroaig, Brown-Forman to Slane, Glenmorangie's designer casks) is the specific story.
   Options: keep it as a beginner primer (then the items below are small fixes), or cut slides 2 to 4
   and 7 to 9 to one or two and spend the time on the ownership history (Hiram Walker, Allied,
   Fortune Brands, Beam Suntory, Suntory Global Spirits), Cairdeas Warehouse 1, Brown-Forman's
   cooperage feeding Slane, and Glenmorangie's designer casks. Not done: it changes the deck's
   content and slide count.

## A. Facts and hedges

2. **Slide 1 (slide hook).**
   Current: "Why most of the world’s whisky is aged in barrels that held bourbon first."
   Proposed: "Why most Scotch is aged in barrels that held bourbon first."
   Why: the outline establishes only that Scotch estimates "cluster around 90%"; "the world's whisky" includes bourbon itself, Canadian, Japanese and Indian whisky (R should-fix S5).

3. **Slide 1 (script), paragraph 2.**
   Current: "So every barrel in Kentucky gets used once, for bourbon, and then it has to go somewhere else."
   Proposed: "So every bourbon barrel gets used once for bourbon, and then it has to go somewhere else."
   Why: not every barrel in Kentucky holds bourbon (R S5).

4. **Slide 2 (script), paragraph 1.**
   Current: "Almost every bourbon barrel is American white oak, Quercus alba..."
   Proposed: "The standard wood for a bourbon barrel is American white oak, Quercus alba..."
   Why: the outline says "the standard bourbon barrel wood", not "almost every" (R minor M12). Optional.

5. **Slide 3 (slide row "Hoop") and script.**
   Current, slide: "drive iron hoops down to hold it". Script: "inside a metal hoop".
   Proposed, slide: "drive steel hoops down to hold it" (or "drive hoops down to hold it").
   Why: modern hoops are galvanised steel and the slide and script disagree (R minor M5). The outline does not name the metal, so "hoops" alone is the safe choice.

6. **Slide 4 (slide) against the script.**
   Current, slide: Red layer "Wood sugars caramelised by heat. Vanillin, colour." Oak "Tannin and oak lactones." Script: vanillin and lactones both come "from the red layer and just behind it".
   Proposed, slide: Red layer "Wood sugars caramelised by heat. Vanillin, lactones, colour." Oak "Tannin."
   Why: the slide and script place the lactones differently; the outline says vanillin comes mainly from lignin and does not place it in a layer (R minor M2). Either align the slide to the script or soften the script.

7. **Slide 5 (script), paragraph 2.**
   Current: "Accounts disagree on the day it took effect, 1 March or 1 July 1938, which is why the slide says only \"by 1938\"."
   Proposed: "Sources disagree on whether it took effect on 1 March or 1 July 1938."
   Why: the host should not talk about the slide's wording as research (R should-fix S8).

8. **Slide 6 (script), paragraph 2.**
   Current: "I couldn't find a current statement from either company that every barrel behind the ten-year-old is ex-Maker's Mark, so I'd say \"largely\" rather than \"all\"."
   Proposed: "Neither company says every barrel behind the ten-year-old is ex-Maker's Mark, so \"largely\" is the honest word."
   Why: the researcher's first person ends up in the host's mouth (R S8).

9. **Slide 7 (slide strip).**
   Current: "Maker’s Mark moves its barrels between floors by hand."
   Proposed: "Maker’s Mark says it moves its barrels between floors by hand."
   Why: found. The outline has it only as a company statement ("The company says it rotates its barrels").

10. **Slide 7 (script), paragraph 3.**
    Current: "Maker's Mark says it still rotates its barrels between floors by hand, which most distillers gave up long ago."
    Proposed: "Maker's Mark says it still rotates its barrels between floors by hand."
    Why: "which most distillers gave up long ago" is not in the outline (R minor M1).

11. **Slide 9 (slide, green card).**
    Current: "New barrel. Most of the colour, vanilla and tannin, taken quickly."
    Proposed: "New barrel. Much of the colour, vanilla and tannin, taken quickly."
    Why: the script says "the colour and a large share of the vanilla and the tannin" (R minor M3).

12. **Slide 9 (script), paragraph 3.**
    Current: "That's one reason Scotch often spends ten or twelve years in a used barrel."
    Proposed: cut the sentence, or "That may be part of why Scotch often spends ten or twelve years in a used barrel."
    Why: the causal link is not in the outline (R minor M1).

13. **Slide 10 (slide row "How they travel").**
    Current: "Now mostly whole. Until the 1980s, as bundles of staves."
    Proposed: "Now mostly whole. Until about the late 1980s, as bundles of staves."
    Why: found. The outline and the script say "until about the late 1980s"; "until the 1980s" reads as ending in 1980.

14. **Slide 12 (slide card "Refill") against the script.**
    Current, slide: "Less again. Often two or three times." Script: "Some distilleries fill a cask three or four times."
    Proposed, slide: "Less again. Often refilled more than once." Script: "Some distilleries fill a cask several times."
    Why: the outline gives no count, and the two disagree (R minor M1).

15. **Slide 14 (script), paragraph 2.**
    Current: "How much of its standard whisky goes into them, as opposed to barrels bought on the open market, isn't something I've been able to pin down."
    Proposed: "Glenmorangie doesn't say how much of its standard whisky goes into them, as opposed to barrels bought on the open market."
    Why: researcher's voice (R S8).

16. **Slide 3 (credit) against the outline.**
    Current, slide: "...Jean-François Millet, about 1850." Outline: "about 1848–52".
    Proposed: keep the slide, or "about 1848–52" on both.
    Why: found. Small mismatch between caption and credit; the slide credit was kept word for word.

17. **Slide 1 (credit).** The caption "Bourbon barrels in a Kentucky warehouse" follows the Commons title ("Lincoln Heritage Scenic Highway - Bourbon Barrels in a Local Warehouse"); the NARA record 7720083 was never opened (outline Not confirmed; R minor M11).
