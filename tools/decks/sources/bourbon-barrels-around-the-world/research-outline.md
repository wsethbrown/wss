# Bourbon Barrels Around the World

Research outline: The bourbon barrel abroad

The bourbon barrel abroad. A guide for anyone who wants to research the subject and build a talk of their own.

---

**A note on confidence.** The facts in the deck were checked in November 2026 against the sources listed under each module. Where sources disagree, the module says so and the deck uses the better-supported version. What could not be confirmed is listed under "Not confirmed".

## Module 1. The tree

**The question.** Why are whiskey barrels made from white oak, and where does the wood come from?

### Nailed down

- American white oak (_Quercus alba_) is the standard bourbon barrel wood. Much of the stave timber comes from Missouri and the Ozarks; Independent Stave, the largest barrel maker, is in Lebanon, Missouri.
- White oak's vessels are blocked by tyloses; a 2024 X-ray study found about seven times the tylose volume of northern red oak. Red oak barrels leak.
- Because American white oak has so many tyloses, its staves can be sawn. European oak must be split along the grain, which uses much less of the log.
- Staves are seasoned outdoors before use; cooperages quote from three to six months up to about two years.

### Primary documents

- U.S. Forest Service, Forest Products Laboratory, _Wood Handbook_ (free).
- Kim et al., _Scientific Reports_, 2024, on tyloses in oak species.

### Traps

- The age of trees felled for staves (often given as 60 to 100 years) was not confirmed; the deck gives no figure.
- University of Missouri Extension and the Missouri Department of Conservation give different annual barrel counts (1.5 million and 2.5 million). Neither is in the deck.

## Module 2. The cooper and the char

**The question.** How is a barrel built, and what does fire do to the inside of it?

### Nailed down

- A barrel is held together by hoops and the swelling of wet wood. Brown-Forman's cooperage aims for 29 to 33 staves; a 53-gallon barrel carries six hoops.
- Staves are raised inside a hoop, heated, drawn together, hooped, and the heads are set into a groove (the croze).
- Toasting heats the wood without ignition. Charring burns the inner surface. Char is sold as No. 1 to No. 4; Independent Stave calls its No. 4 "alligator char". Commonly quoted times run from about 15 seconds for No. 1 to about 55 for No. 4, and vary by cooperage.
- Heat breaks hemicellulose into wood sugars that caramelize; vanillin comes mainly from lignin; American oak is high in oak lactones (coconut). Heavier char reduces the lactone effect.

### Primary documents

- Independent Stave Company and Brown-Forman Cooperage published specifications.
- Peer-reviewed reviews of oak-derived flavor compounds in aged spirits.

### Traps

- "Red layer" is used two ways: by coopers for the caramelized band behind the char, and in a 2018 _Scientific Reports_ paper for the line showing how far spirit has soaked in. The deck uses the coopers' sense.
- Claims such as "60 to 70 percent of the flavour comes from the barrel" cannot be measured cleanly. Avoid them.

## Module 3. The new-barrel rule

**The question.** Why must bourbon use a new barrel, and who wanted that?

### Nailed down

- National Prohibition ended in December 1933. The Federal Alcohol Administration Act was approved on 29 August 1935 (49 Stat. 977).
- Standards of identity were published under it. An April 1936 Federal Register notice defined bourbon without the word "new"; the 1938 regulations required storage in charred new oak containers (Lazar, _In the American Grain_, from the Federal Register texts).
- The effective date in 1938 is reported as 1 March (Veach) or 1 July (VinePair). The slide says "by 1938".
- TTB Industry Circular 76-28 (6 December 1976) restates that bourbon must be stored in new charred oak cooperage.
- Current rule (27 CFR 5.143): at least 51% corn, distilled at no more than 160 proof, stored at no more than 125 proof in charred new oak containers. Straight bourbon: at least two years. Bourbon may not contain added coloring, flavoring or blending materials (TTB's interpretation of 5.23).

### Primary documents

- Federal Alcohol Administration Act, 49 Stat. 977 (govinfo.gov).
- Federal Register, 1936–1938, standards of identity for distilled spirits.
- 27 CFR Part 5 (ecfr.gov).

### Traps

- The coopers' and timber lobby story is repeated everywhere and no one has produced the papers. The version crediting Wilbur Mills of Arkansas cannot be right: Mills entered Congress on 3 January 1939.
- The law says "containers", not barrels, and sets no size.

## Module 4. The first fill

**The question.** What happens to the barrel during its years as bourbon?

### Nailed down

- The standard American whiskey barrel holds 53 US gallons, about 200 liters. The size is custom, not law.
- Temperature swings push spirit into and out of the wood. Upper floors of a rickhouse are hotter and drier; there proof usually rises because water escapes faster than alcohol. In Scotland's cool, damp warehouses strength falls.
- The angels' share in Scotland averages about 2% a year, the figure Scottish Government officials have also used.
- Maker's Mark (Loretto, Kentucky) is a wheated bourbon (red winter wheat) at 90 proof, 45% ABV. The company says it rotates its barrels between floors by hand.
- Jim Beam Devil's Cut is made by extracting bourbon held in the wood of emptied six-year-old barrels and blending it with extra-aged Jim Beam (company launch release, 2011).

### Primary documents

- Maker's Mark's own published account of its process.
- Jim Beam's 2011 launch release for Devil's Cut.

### Traps

- The change from 48 to 53 gallons during the Second World War is told by Angel's Envy, Lew Bryson and others; one of them doubts the details. The deck calls it trade lore.
- One 2024 Veach post describes the floors the other way round; the other sources and the mechanism support hot, dry upper floors.

## Module 5. Export and the second fill

**The question.** Where do used bourbon barrels go, and how do they get there?

### Nailed down

- Scotch whisky must be matured in an excise warehouse in Scotland, in oak casks of no more than 700 liters, for at least three years; only water and plain caramel coloring (E150A) may be added. New wood is not required. Estimates of the share matured at least partly in ex-bourbon casks cluster around 90%.
- Irish whiskey must be matured for at least three years in wooden casks in the Republic, Northern Ireland or both; the technical file sets a 700-liter limit.
- Until about the late 1980s most barrels were shipped to Scotland as shooks (bundles of staves) and rebuilt; today they mostly travel whole. Many are rebuilt into hogsheads of about 250 liters.
- Ex-bourbon barrels are the usual cask for aged tequila (one estimate: 70–80%) and for much Caribbean rum.
- Casks are refilled; a first-fill cask gives the most flavor, refills progressively less. Spent casks may be rejuvenated (scraped and re-charred).
- Laphroaig went to Allied in 1989; Maker's Mark had gone to Allied with Hiram Walker in 1987. Both passed to Fortune Brands (Beam) in 2005, then Beam Suntory in 2014, now Suntory Global Spirits.
- Laphroaig's 2022 Cairdeas Warehouse 1 was matured entirely in first-fill ex-Maker's Mark casks. Third-party accounts say the 10-year-old's ex-bourbon casks come mostly from Maker's Mark; no current official statement that all of them do was found, so the deck says "largely".
- Laphroaig 10 is 86 proof (43% ABV) in the US, 750 ml; 40% in some other markets.

### Primary documents

- The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 (legislation.gov.uk).
- Irish Whiskey Act 1980 (irishstatutebook.ie) and the Irish whiskey technical file.

### Traps

- Rum and tequila regulations do not require ex-bourbon wood; it is common practice, not law.

## Module 6. Ireland, and the barrel built for Scotland

**The question.** How do other producers treat the ex-bourbon barrel, and has anyone reversed the arrangement?

### Nailed down

- Slane Irish Whiskey (Slane Castle, County Meath) is owned by Brown-Forman, which also owns Jack Daniel's and Woodford Reserve and has its own cooperages. Brown-Forman bought the business in 2015 and built a distillery at the castle.
- Slane is "triple casked": virgin American oak, "seasoned" casks that previously held Tennessee whiskey and bourbon, and Oloroso sherry casks, blended before bottling. 40% ABV, sold in the US in 750 ml.
- Glenmorangie (Tain) describes "designer casks": oak from the Ozark Mountains of Missouri, air-seasoned for about two years, lent to American distillers before shipping to Scotland. Jack Daniel's and Heaven Hill are the distillers usually named; the loan is usually given as four years, though a 2008 _Whisky Magazine_ article gave four to eight.
- Glenmorangie The Original became a 12-year-old in July 2024, replacing the 10-year-old introduced in the 1960s. 40% ABV. The company says it is aged in bourbon casks; it is on sale in the US in 750 ml.

### Primary documents

- Glenmorangie's own published accounts of its wood program, and interviews with its director of whisky creation, Bill Lumsden.
- Brown-Forman's brand page for Slane.

### Traps

- Reports that Slane's seasoned casks are mostly ex-Jack Daniel's rest on press coverage and inference from ownership; the company says "Tennessee whiskey and bourbon" without proportions.
- How much of The Original comes from designer casks rather than casks bought on the open market is not disclosed. Retailers describe the 10-year-old as first- and second-fill ex-bourbon; this was not confirmed for the 12, so the deck says only "ex-bourbon".
- Older bottles and pages still say "10 years old". Buy the 12.

## The four pours

- **Pour 1. Maker's Mark.** A bourbon from a new charred barrel, whose barrels go to Laphroaig.
- **Pour 2. Laphroaig 10.** The second fill of ex-Maker's Mark barrels.
- **Pour 3. Slane Irish Whiskey.** Irish whiskey whose owner, Brown-Forman, sends its own used Tennessee whiskey and bourbon barrels to Ireland.
- **Pour 4. Glenmorangie The Original 12.** A Scotch distillery that buys the tree and lends the barrel to America first.

Producers, cask policy and availability change. Check each bottle's own label and the producer's current description of its casks before the night. Prices vary too much by region to quote.

## Not confirmed

- **Laphroaig 10 casks all ex-Maker's Mark.** Searched for statements by Laphroaig and Maker's Mark; found only the 2022 Cairdeas release and third-party accounts. The deck says "largely". A current statement on laphroaig.com or from Suntory Global Spirits would settle it.
- **Slane's seasoned casks mostly ex-Jack Daniel's.** Press reports only; the company says "Tennessee whiskey and bourbon". The script says so. Brown-Forman's technical sheet would settle it.
- **The 48-to-53-gallon change in the Second World War.** Only trade sources; the script calls it trade lore. Cooperage records or wartime War Production Board orders would settle it.
- **The exact 1938 date** (1 March or 1 July). The slide says "by 1938". The Federal Register issue that published the 1938 regulations would settle it.
- **Rickhouse photograph on the cover.** The National Archives record (7720083) was not opened; the caption follows its Commons title.
