# Hot Climate Whisky

Research outline: Single malt from India and Taiwan

Single malt from India and Taiwan. A guide for anyone who wants to research the subject and build a talk of their own.

**Sources.** The facts in this deck were checked in November 2026 against the sources listed under each module. ## Module 1. What India calls whisky

**The question.** Why is so much Indian whisky not whisky in Europe, and what is in the bottles?

### Nailed down

- India is the world's largest whisky market by consumption volume (IWSR, as reported); most of it is Indian whisky. India is also the largest export market for Scotch by volume (SWA, 2024 and 2025 figures).
- Most Indian whisky is made from molasses-based neutral spirit, blended with a proportion of malt or grain whisky and colored.
- The legal standard is the Food Safety and Standards (Alcoholic Beverages) Regulations, 2018 (FSSAI). Whisky may be made from neutral spirit of agricultural origin, and the general definition of a distilled beverage lists molasses as a source. A blended whisky needs at least 2% malt or grain whisky. The Bureau of Indian Standards also publishes IS 4449 (current edition 2024), a voluntary specification for whisky.
- "Indian-made foreign liquor" (IMFL) is the official excise term for spirits made in India after the manner of imported gin, brandy, whisky or rum (Andhra Pradesh distillery rules; CAG glossary). Rules vary by state; Uttar Pradesh's 2026–27 policy reportedly restricts molasses spirit to country liquor and rum.

### Primary documents

- Bureau of Indian Standards, the standard for whisky (IS 4449).
- Annual market reports from IWSR or the Scotch Whisky Association on volume by country.
- Company annual reports of the large Indian producers (United Spirits, Allied Blenders, John Distilleries).

### Traps

- Volume and value tell different stories. India is usually said to lead on volume; it does not lead on money spent.
- "Indian whisky is fake" is a common sneer. It is whisky under Indian rules, which are a legitimate local definition.
- Grain-based Indian whisky exists and is growing. Do not say all of it is molasses.

## Module 2. The European definition

**The question.** Why can most Indian whisky not be sold as whisky in the European Union?

### Nailed down

- Regulation (EU) 2019/787, Annex I, point 2: whisky is produced by distilling a mash made from malted cereals, with or without whole grains of unmalted cereals; the final distillate is matured at least three years in wooden casks of no more than 700 liters; minimum strength 40%; no added alcohol. It replaced Regulation (EC) No 110/2008.
- "Single malt" may be used only for whisky distilled exclusively from malted barley at a single distillery.
- An age or maturation period on the label may refer only to the youngest alcoholic component (Regulation 2019/787, recital 15 and Article 13; for Scotch, the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009, regulation 12).
- The same definition applies to whisky made in or imported into the UK (HMRC).
- Spirit made from molasses does not meet the cereal requirement.

### Sources

- Regulation (EU) 2019/787, Annex I (legislation.gov.uk): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2019/787/annexes/adopted
- Regulation (EU) 2019/787 (EUR-Lex): https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/787/oj/eng
- HMRC, Spirits Production Manual SPIR3250, whisk(e)y definitions: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/spirits-production/spir3250
- The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009, regulation 12: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/2890/regulation/12
- European Parliament Legislative Observatory, summary of the 2019 spirit drinks regulation: https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-document-summary/pdf?id=1584389

### Primary documents

- Regulation (EU) 2019/787, Annex I, category 2 (free, EUR-Lex).
- The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 (UK), for the Scottish definition of single malt and age statements.

### Traps

- The UK left the EU in 2020. Check the current UK instrument before saying "EU" about a British shelf.
- The Scotch Whisky Association's interest is commercial as well as definitional. Say so.

## Module 3. Amrut and the Glasgow launch, 2004

**The question.** Why did an Indian distiller launch its single malt in Scotland?

### Nailed down

- Amrut Distilleries was founded in Bangalore in 1948 by J. N. Radhakrishna Rao Jagdale. One account dates an earlier family firm, Amrut Laboratories, to 1947.
- His son Neelakanta Rao Jagdale joined in 1972 and became chairman and managing director after Radhakrishna's death in 1976.
- Amrut single malt was launched in Glasgow on 24 August 2004, at an Indian restaurant (given as "Cafe India" in one account and "the Indian Café" in another).
- The launch is credited to the founder's grandson, Rakshit Jagdale, later executive director. As an MBA student in Newcastle in the 1990s he had taken a sample of Amrut's malt spirit to the owner of the Pot Still bar in Glasgow. The first new make for the single malt was in 2002.
- The reason the company gives is that there was no home market for an expensive Indian malt at the time.
- Jim Murray's _Whisky Bible 2010_ gave Amrut Fusion 97 points and placed it third in the world, behind an American and a Scotch whisky. Several independent outlets report this; the book itself was not consulted.

### Primary documents

- Amrut's own company history and press releases.
- Trade press coverage from 2004 to 2010 (_Whisky Magazine_, _The Spirits Business_).
- Jim Murray, _Whisky Bible 2010_.

### Traps

- The story of bottles first going to Indian restaurants in Britain is widely repeated and thinly documented. Present it as company lore.
- Murray's ratings are one critic's opinions. Do not present them as an award.

## Module 4. Heat, evaporation and maturation

**The question.** What does a hot warehouse do to a cask, and what does it cost?

### Nailed down

- The Scotch Whisky Association puts average losses from Scottish casks at around 2% a year. Losses are higher in a cask's first years (The Glenlivet gives 3.5–4%).
- Amrut's own figures for Bangalore: a Jagdale (given only as "Amrut's founder" in the source) quoted at "about 12 per cent"; Rakshit Jagdale more recently at 9–10%, with 13–15% on the coast and in north India.
- Paul John gives 8–10% a year for Goa on its own website.
- Kavalan figures vary widely by source. Its US importer, Hotaling & Co., gives at least 15% a year on the top floors of one warehouse; other outlets give 12–15%. No figure from Kavalan itself was found.
- Amrut's master blender, Surinder Kumar, is reported as estimating that a year in India equals three in Scotland. Kavalan's master blender Ian Chang has said a year in Taiwan equals four or five in cooler climates. Both are company estimates.
- Slide 8 assumptions: a constant loss of 2% a year (Scotland, SWA average) and 10% a year (hot warehouse, inside Amrut's 9–12% and Paul John's 8–10%), compounded. Remaining: 92%, 85%, 78% after 4, 8, 12 years in Scotland; 66%, 43%, 28% in the hot warehouse. At twelve years 78/28 ≈ 2.8 casks for each Scottish one.

### Primary documents

- Distillery statements (Amrut, Paul John, Kavalan) on their own losses. These are the only figures available; treat them as company figures.
- Scientific literature on cask maturation and temperature (for example, the _Journal of the Institute of Brewing_).

### Traps

- Loss is not constant from year to year; the compounding on slide 8 is an illustration.
- In dry heat alcohol tends to stay strong as water evaporates; in humid heat strength can fall. The direction depends on the warehouse.

## Module 5. Paul John, Goa

**The question.** How did a second Indian mass-market whisky company come to make malt?

### Nailed down

- Paul John single malt is made by John Distilleries, the company of Paul P. John, based in Bangalore. Its best-known brand is Original Choice, which the company calls the world's sixth-largest whisky brand.
- The company began making malt in Goa in 2008. Its first single malt, Single Cask 161, was launched at the Capital Hotel, Knightsbridge, London, on 4 October 2012, before release in India.
- Paul John Brilliance: unpeated, Indian six-row barley, ex-bourbon American oak, 46%, non-chill-filtered. Sold in the US (US importer named in 2019 as 375 Park Avenue Spirits; listed at US retailers at about $58–65).
- The malt distillery is at Cuncolim in south Goa, about 15 km from Mobor beach.
- The company gives its angels' share as 8–10% a year.

### Primary documents

- John Distilleries' corporate history.
- Trade press from 2012 onward.

### Traps

- The founding year is disputed: 1992 in several sources, 1996 on the company's own site.
- Claims about what Indian six-row barley tastes of are hard to separate from climate and casks. Avoid them.

## Module 6. Kavalan, Taiwan

**The question.** How did a private whisky distillery come to exist in Taiwan, and how did it become known so fast?

### Nailed down

- Taiwan's alcohol market was de-monopolized in 2002, when Taiwan joined the World Trade Organization.
- King Car Group was founded in 1979 by Lee Tien-tsai. Its plan to enter the alcohol business in 1995 was refused by the government.
- King Car broke ground on the Kavalan distillery in Yilan County in 2005. A King Car representative dates the first new make to 3.30pm on 11 March 2006. (One source places first distillation in 2008; the 2006 date is better supported.)
- The first bottling, Kavalan Classic, was released in December 2008 (4 December, per Wikipedia).
- The name Kavalan is the old name of the Yilan area and of its indigenous people.
- The Scottish consultant Dr Jim Swan advised on wood and maturation; Kavalan was the first distillery to use his STR (shaved, toasted, re-charred) cask treatment.
- The Leith tasting, January 2010. _The Times_'s Scottish editor asked Charles MacLean to organize a blind comparison of the new English malt St George's with Scotch, for Burns Night. Nothing in the line-up could be older than St George's. It was held at the Vintners' Rooms in Leith; four judges, MacLean as head taster. A Kavalan reported as two years old, not then sold in Britain, won with 27.5 points out of 40. MacLean's own account lists six whiskies: Kavalan 27.5; two three-year-old Scotch blends, 22 and 20 (one named elsewhere as Lang's); St George's 15.5; Scotch malts at 8.5 and 4.5 (the last named in press reports as Bruichladdich X4+3). Press reports describe a field of five.
- At the World Whiskies Awards 2015, Kavalan Solist Vinho Barrique (single cask, cask strength) was named World's Best Single Malt.

### Primary documents

- King Car and Kavalan corporate histories.
- Taiwan's Tobacco and Alcohol Administration Act (2000, in force 2002).
- _The Times_, January 2010, report of the Leith blind tasting.
- World Whiskies Awards results, 2015.

### Traps

- The Leith tasting was small and set up for a newspaper story. Its field was deliberately very young.
- Kavalan's later awards are numerous. Quote one or two with sources rather than a total.

## Module 7. Age statements

**The question.** Why do these distilleries not put an age on their standard bottles?

### Nailed down

- The standard bottlings of Amrut, Paul John and Kavalan in this flight carry no age statement.
- Under Scotch and EU rules a stated age refers to the youngest whisky in the bottle (see Module 2).
- The reasons given are cost (evaporation and storage), the risk of drinkers reading a young age against Scotch, and the companies' argument that time is a poor measure in a hot climate.

### Primary documents

- The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009, on age statements.
- Interviews with Kavalan and Amrut staff on no-age-statement policy (trade press).

### Traps

- The companies' argument is reasonable and also self-serving. Present both.
- "No age statement" does not mean young in every case, and stated ages are not a measure of quality.
