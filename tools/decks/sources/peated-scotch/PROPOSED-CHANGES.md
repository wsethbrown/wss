# Peated Scotch: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/Peat-Reek/`) with its
words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below is a
proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/Peat-Reek.md`, "R" plus its item number) and my own read ("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slide 14 (slide text) and slide 16 (script). Ardbeg's malt source.**
   Current, slide 14: "Peated malt from Port Ellen". Script 16: "since the restart all of it has come from Port Ellen".
   Proposed, slide 14: "Peated malt, reportedly from Port Ellen". Script 16: "third-party sources say it has all come from Port Ellen since the restart".
   Why: the outline's own "Not confirmed" list says no Ardbeg statement was found (R should-fix 4).

2. **Slide 13 (slide strip and script). The 1983 closures.**
   Current, slide: "In 1983 the Distillers Company closed 11 of its 45 malt distilleries." Script: "In 1983 DCL closed eleven of its forty-five malt distilleries".
   Proposed, slide: "In 1983 the Distillers Company closed 11 of its 45 malt distilleries, by most counts." Script: "...closed eleven of its forty-five malt distilleries, by most counts".
   Why: the outline says counts differ (eleven or twelve) (R should-fix 6).

3. **Slide 4 (slide row).**
   Current: "1977 · Its own maltings close." Proposed: "c. 1977 · Its own maltings close." (or "1977 (one source: 1980)").
   Why: the script already says one source gives 1980 (R should-fix 6).

4. **Slide 4 (script), paragraph 4.**
   Current: "The bigger break came after the Second World War. As output rose in the 1960s, distilleries closed their floor maltings and bought from large mechanical maltings, and Scottish Malt Distillers, the Distillers Company's malt arm, stopped using lightly peated malt at all its distilleries except those on Islay and Skye."
   Proposed: "The bigger break came after the Second World War. As output rose in the 1960s, distilleries closed their floor maltings and bought from large mechanical maltings. After the war, Scottish Malt Distillers, the Distillers Company's malt arm, stopped using lightly peated malt at all its distilleries except those on Islay and Skye."
   Why: the outline lists the year of the SMD decision as not confirmed; the current sentence ties it to the 1960s (R should-fix 6).

5. **Slide 1 (script), paragraph 2. Found.**
   Current: "A mainland distillery that gave up peat in the nineteenth century".
   Proposed: "A mainland distillery that gave up peat in the twentieth century".
   Why: slide 4's own script says Barnard found peat the only fuel at Glenmorangie in the mid-1880s, that a history says it used peat until about 1940, and that its maltings closed in 1977. The outline's pours table says "burned peat into the twentieth century".

6. **Slide 1 (slide hook) and deck.json `description`. Found, and R minor 13.**
   Current: "...how four distilleries came to use a great deal of it or none."
   Proposed hook: "Why Scottish distillers dried their malt over peat, and how four distilleries came to use none of it, some, or a great deal."
   Proposed description (the review's draft): "How peat smoke got into Scotch, why the mainland let it go and Islay didn't, told through four distilleries from unpeated Glenmorangie to Ardbeg. Four pours."
   Why: Bowmore and Caol Ila sit in between.

7. **Slide 6 (script). Why Islay kept peat.**
   Current: "The island has a great deal of peat, much of it close to the distilleries, and it had no railway. Coal had to come in by boat, which made it expensive. And once the blending trade had grown, the blenders had a use for Islay's smoky malts."
   Proposed: either add sources for these to Module 4 of the outline, or open with "As it's usually told, the island has a great deal of peat...".
   Why: no source in the outline (R should-fix 5). The slide rows (Fuel, Coal, Buyers) carry the same claims.

8. **Slide 15 (script), paragraph 1.**
   Current: "By the late 1990s heavily peated Islay malts had gone from the bit you added to a blend to something people sought out on their own."
   Proposed: "As it's usually told, by the late 1990s heavily peated Islay malts had gone..." or add a source to Module 8.
   Why: Module 8 asks this question but does not answer it (R should-fix 5).

9. **Slide 17 (slide and script). "No legal definition".**
   Proposed: add the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 to the outline as the source, read for this; wording unchanged.
   Why: true as far as the reviewer knows, but no source is cited (R should-fix 5).

10. **Slides 9 and 17 (slide and script). Smoke fades in the cask.**
    Current, slide 9: "Smoke fades with each year in wood." Slide 17: "Smoke fades in the cask. Younger usually tastes smokier."
    Proposed: add a source to Module 5, or slide 9: "Smoke usually fades with each year in wood."
    Why: not in the outline (R should-fix 5).

11. **Slide 8 (slide note and deck.json pour 2 `notes`).**
    Current: "Taste it beside the Glenmorangie: same age and strength, different kiln."
    Proposed: "Taste it beside the Glenmorangie: same age and strength, different smoke."
    Why: the script rightly says the stills and casks differ too; "different kiln" implies the kiln is the only difference (R minor 8).

12. **Slide 12 (slide note and deck.json pour 3 `notes`).**
    Current: "Notice how dry the smoke is next to the Bowmore."
    Proposed: "Is the smoke drier than the Bowmore's?"
    Why: the slide tells the room what to taste; the script hedges ("What people often notice") (R minor 8).

13. **Slide 11 (slide).**
    Current: "At Port Askaig, facing Jura across the Sound of Islay." and the label "destination".
    Proposed: "Near Port Askaig, facing Jura across the Sound of Islay." and the label "where it goes".
    Why: the script and outline say "near"; "destination" reads awkwardly (R minor 7).

14. **Slide 2 (slide row). Found.**
    Current: "The moss · slabs spread out to dry in the wind".
    Proposed: "The moss · slabs thrown out on the moss to dry".
    Why: the outline and script say the slabs were cast onto the moss to dry; "in the wind" is not in either.

15. **Slide 2 (photo credit on the slide).**
    Current: "Peat cutters, from J. M. W. Turner’s print “Peat Bog, Scotland”, 1812. National Gallery of Art, public domain."
    Proposed: "Peat cutters, from “Peat Bog, Scotland” by J. M. W. Turner and George Clint, 1812. National Gallery of Art, public domain."
    Why: the outline's image credit names both (R minor 3).
