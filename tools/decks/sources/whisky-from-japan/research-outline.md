# Whisky From Japan

Research outline: Hakata, koji, and the 2021 definition of Japanese whisky

_This is the other half of the speaker notes. It does not tell you what to say; it tells you where to look, what is settled, and what is still loose. Facts were checked in November 2026. Prices, the legal status of the Japanese whisky standard and Hakata's US availability move, so check them again before a night._

> **THE SPINE OF THE STORY**
> 1853–54: Perry opens Japan, and his expedition brings American whiskey as gifts → 1918–20: Masataka Taketsuru studies in Glasgow, apprentices at Longmorn and Hazelburn, marries Rita Cowan → 1923: Torii Shinjiro builds Yamazaki, with Taketsuru → 1929: first Yamazaki whisky on sale → 1934: Taketsuru leaves and founds the company that becomes Nikka, at Yoichi → 2001 and 2015: world awards and the boom → February 2021: the trade body defines "Japanese whisky", requiring malted grain → 2023: Hakata, a koji-made barley spirit from a Fukuoka shochu distillery, launches in the US as whisky from Japan.

## How to use this

Modules 1 to 4 are the history the room hears while drinking the first glass. Module 5 is the standard. Modules 6 and 7 are the turn: what Hakata is and why Japanese law and the standard leave it out. Module 8 has the bottles. Then the claims that do not hold up, what could not be confirmed, sources and image credits.

The standard of proof: a fact in "What's established" was found in a source opened for this deck. Where only one source says it, that is stated. Anything weaker is in "Not confirmed" and the script says it aloud as a story or leaves it out.

## Module 1. Perry and the first whisky

_How did whisky reach a country that had been closed for two centuries?_

### What's established

- **8 July 1853.** Commodore Matthew Perry's squadron enters Uraga Bay. He returns in 1854 and the Convention of Kanagawa is signed on 31 March 1854 (nomunication.jp timeline, translated from the Japan Whisky Research Centre).
- **1854.** Gifts from Perry's expedition to Japanese officials included casks of whiskey; the same timeline calls this the first written record of Japanese people drinking whisky in Japan, while noting it was probably not the first time any Japanese person had tasted it.
- **c. 1870.** Wikipedia's Japanese whisky article dates the introduction of whisky to Japan to about 1870, with commercial production from 1923.

### Go to the source

- › _Liam McNulty, "How the Japan Was Won: Commodore Perry and Japan's First Whiskies", nomunication.jp, 26 June 2023: a dated timeline of the expedition with the whisky gifts, from the Japan Whisky Research Centre._
- › _Francis L. Hawks, Narrative of the Expedition of an American Squadron to the China Seas and Japan (1856): the expedition's own account, quoted in the timeline above. Not read for this deck._

> **TRAPS**
> "Whisky arrived with the traders who followed" is true but incomplete: Perry's own ships brought it. Quantities and recipients of the gift casks vary between accounts; do not quote a number.

## Module 2. Torii, Taketsuru and Yamazaki

_Who built Japanese whisky, and why did the two men part?_

### What's established

- **1899.** Torii Shinjiro opens Torii Shoten in Osaka to sell imported wines (Wikipedia, Suntory). His Akadama port wine came in 1907.
- **1918–1920.** Taketsuru, from a sake-brewing family in Takehara, Hiroshima, goes to Scotland in 1918, enrols at the University of Glasgow, apprentices at Longmorn (April 1919) and at Hazelburn in Campbeltown (1920), and returns to Japan in November 1920 (Wikipedia, Masataka Taketsuru and Japanese whisky).
- **1923.** Yamazaki distillery opens under Torii's Kotobukiya, Japan's first commercial whisky distillery; Taketsuru is factory director from 1924 (Wikipedia, Yamazaki distillery).
- **1929.** Suntory Shirofuda, the first whisky made in Japan, goes on sale.
- **1 March 1934.** Taketsuru leaves Kotobukiya. Japanese Wikipedia gives as reasons that successors had been trained and that the original promise of ten years had run out. On 2 July 1934 he founds Dai Nippon Kaju at Yoichi, Hokkaido, later Nikka; Nikka's first whisky came out in 1940.
- Yoichi was chosen for a cool climate that reminded Taketsuru of Scotland.

### Go to the source

- › _Olive Checkland, Japanese Whisky, Scotch Blend (Scottish Cultural Press, 1998): the fullest account of Taketsuru and Rita. Not read for this deck._
- › _Wikipedia (English and Japanese), Masataka Taketsuru, Yamazaki distillery, Japanese whisky: maps to the Nikka and Suntory corporate histories they cite. Read in part._

> **TRAPS**
> Yamazaki is in Shimamoto, Osaka Prefecture, between Kyoto and Osaka; "outside Kyoto" is fair, "in Kyoto" is not. The ten-year contract appears in Japanese sources as a promise, not a document anyone has published.

## Module 3. Rita

_What did Rita Cowan actually do?_

### What's established

- **14 December 1896.** Jessie Roberta Cowan is born in Kirkintilloch, near Glasgow, a doctor's daughter (nippon.com; Wikipedia).
- **January 1920.** She marries Taketsuru at a register office against both families; they leave for Japan in November 1920 (nippon.com; Wikipedia).
- In Osaka she taught English and piano at Tezukayama Gakuin (Japanese Wikipedia). The Lady says she taught English for six years from about 1923, and that investors for Taketsuru's own company were found through her English pupil, the wife of the businessman Shotaro Kaga (one source).
- **1988.** Strathkelvin council, whose district included Kirkintilloch, twinned with Yoichi (nippon.com). The road in front of Yoichi station is named Rita Road (Wikipedia).
- She is widely called the mother of Japanese whisky, a name better known in Japan than in Scotland. She died at Yoichi on 17 January 1961.

### Go to the source

- › _Tony McNicol, "The Woman Who Took Whisky to Japan: Remembering Rita Cowan Taketsuru", nippon.com: Kirkintilloch, the family, the twinning. Read in full._
- › _"The Scottish mother of Japanese whisky", The Lady (lady.co.uk): her teaching and the Kaga connection. Read in full._

> **TRAPS**
> English Wikipedia says Rita studied chemistry at Glasgow University; Japanese Wikipedia puts the chemistry with Masataka and has her studying music and literature, and nippon.com says it was her sister Ella who was at the university. The slide calls her a doctor's daughter instead. "She told him to go and build his own" is the popular story; it is said aloud as a story.

## Module 5. The 2021 standard

_What does "Japanese whisky" mean, and who enforces it?_

### What's established

- **12 February 2021.** The Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association (JSLMA) establishes the Standards for Labeling Japanese Whisky; published 16 February (Whisky Advocate).
- The requirements, from JSLMA's English text: raw ingredients limited to malted grains, other cereal grains and water extracted in Japan, and malted grains must always be used; saccharification, fermentation and distillation at a distillery in Japan, distilled below 95%; matured in Japan in wooden casks of no more than 700 litres for at least three years; bottled only in Japan at 40% or more; plain caramel allowed.
- **1 April 2021.** In force. Products already sold with these terms before 31 March 2021 could keep their labels until **31 March 2024**.
- It is voluntary, applies to members, and has no government enforcement (Whisky Advocate). JSLMA had 104 members in March 2025.
- **27 March 2025.** JSLMA adopts a "JW" logo for compliant bottles and says it is working towards a legally binding definition: a Geographical Indication and a National Tax Agency labelling standard. A document on its site dated January 2026 repeats this; no sign was found that either has been adopted.
- JSLMA's own note on the standard names two problems: imported whisky sold as Japanese whisky, and products that do not meet the Japanese tax-law definition of whisky being sold as whisky abroad. Article 6.3 asks members not to supply products labelled that way.

### Go to the source

- › _JSLMA, Standards for Labeling Japanese Whisky, and Regarding standards for labeling Japanese whisky (yoshu.or.jp, English PDFs): the text itself. Read in full._
- › _Susannah Skiver Barton, "Japanese Whisky Finally Gets a Definition, But It's Complicated", Whisky Advocate, 22 February 2021. Read in full._
- › _JSLMA press release, 27 March 2025, on the JW logo and GI plans. Read in full._

> **TRAPS**
> The slide lists five rules; the standard also caps cask size at 700 litres and distillation strength below 95%. "Full compliance 31 March 2024" is the end of the transition for existing labels. Check whether the standard has become law before you present.

## Module 6. Koji and the law in Japan

_Why can't Hakata be called whisky at home?_

### What's established

- Japan's Liquor Tax Act (Act No. 6 of 1953), Article 3, item 15, defines whisky as distilled from grain saccharified by malted grain (e-Gov text). Item 10 defines single-distilled shochu as a pot-still spirit from grain, its koji and water, at 45% or less.
- Koji is a mould grown on grain that converts starch to sugar; it is used for sake, miso and soy sauce (Punch; Hakata's importer).
- Cask-aged koji spirits in Japan have to be stripped of colour or blended with unaged spirit to be sold as shochu; Punch gives the limit as a reading of 0.08 or lower on a spectrometer.
- In the US, whisky is a distillate from a fermented mash of any grain, below 95%, stored in oak, bottled at 40% or more (27 CFR 5.143). It does not ask how the starch was converted.
- The JSLMA standard's malted-grain rule shuts koji whisky out of "Japanese whisky" (Punch, 2021).

### Go to the source

- › _Liquor Tax Act, Article 3, via e-Gov (laws.e-gov.go.jp, law ID 328AC0000000006): the definitions of shochu and whisky, in Japanese. Read in part._
- › _Susannah Skiver Barton, "Japan's Forgotten Koji Whisky", Punch, 2021: koji whisky between the two categories. Read in full._
- › _27 CFR 5.143, via Cornell LII. Read in part._

### Threads worth pulling

- Jokichi Takamine worked on a koji-based process for American whiskey in the 1890s (Wikipedia, Japanese whisky). It would make a good aside about how old the idea is.

> **TRAPS**
> The original slide said koji drinks and Western drinks had been separate tax categories "since 1896". Japan's 1896 laws were the Sake Brewing Tax Act and the Mixed Liquor Tax Act; no source was found for that exact claim, so the slide now cites the current Act instead. One review says tax law keeps Hakata out of Japan on strength; the Act caps shochu at 45% and has no such minimum, so the claim is left out.

## Module 7. Hikari and Hakata

_What is in the glass?_

### What's established

- Hakata is made by Hikari Shuzo (the Hikari Distillery), Hakata-ku, Fukuoka, founded **1912**, a barley shochu maker (Whisky Advocate, 2023; brand site). Whisky Advocate notes it is a different company from Hikari Distillery Ltd, which opened north of Tokyo in 2020.
- 100% barley. Skurnik, the distributor, gives the recipe as two thirds unmalted barley and one third white koji barley, parallel fermented for two weeks; single distilled in stainless pot stills; aged in ex-oloroso sherry casks; non-chill filtered; 42%.
- The barley is from Kyushu (Whiskey Wash reviews).
- The casks sit in traditional and open-air warehouses; Fukuoka runs from about 95°F in summer to about 38°F in winter (brand site; Whisky Advocate).
- The casks were bought in Spain about twenty years ago by the then president, Naoki Mitsuya (brand site).
- **20 June 2023.** US launch through ImpEx Beverages of the 10, 12, 16 and 18 Year, all 42% (press release).
- It is too dark to sell as shochu and is not sold in Japan (Whiskey Wash, 2023).

### Go to the source

- › _hakatawhisky.com, home, Our Collection, The Origin: the producer's own account. Read in full._
- › _Skurnik Wines & Spirits product pages, Hakata 10 and 16: the recipe. Read in full._
- › _Jonny McCormick, "Hakata: Japanese Single Malt With a Different Twist", Whisky Advocate, 18 July 2023. Read in full._

> **DO NOT OVERSELL IT**
> The brand site calls the 12 "a classic incantation between koji and malt". The distributor says the mash is koji barley and unmalted barley. "No malted barley" rests on the distributor's recipe; say so if asked.

## Claims that don't hold up

- "Rita, a chemistry student at Glasgow": the sources disagree; the deck calls her a doctor's daughter.
- "At 84 proof it falls below the strength Japanese law requires": the Liquor Tax Act sets a maximum of 45% for single-distilled shochu, not a minimum.
- "Koji drinks and Western drinks have been separate tax categories since 1896": not found in any source opened.
- "The tree needs two hundred years, against about eighty for American oak": the two hundred years is supported (Decanter, VinePair); the eighty is not.
- The original map put the Hakushu and Yamazaki markers in the wrong places; the redrawn map uses each distillery's coordinates.

## Not confirmed

- That Hikari is a family firm whose family lives on the grounds. Not found. The script says it as a story; the slide leaves it out. The owner's closing lines on the Sláinte slide still say "a family distillery".
- That Rita told Taketsuru to build his own. Said as "the story goes".
- That Hakata "has never been sold in Japan". The slide says "It is not sold in Japan", from a 2023 review.
- The ten-year contract as a written contract (Japanese sources describe a promise).
- Prices of "forty dollars" rising to "four hundred" in the boom. Said as unconfirmed.
- Mizunara's history: the shortage during and after the Second World War is the "commonly recounted version" (VinePair). Said as the usual telling.
- Whether the JSLMA standard has become law since January 2026.
- The 1896 tax-category claim (see above).
