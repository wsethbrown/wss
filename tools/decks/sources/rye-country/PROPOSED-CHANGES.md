# Rye Country: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/Rye-Country/`) with its
words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below is a
proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/Rye-Country.md`, "R" plus its item number, "R minor" for its minor list) and my
own read of the script against the slides and the outline ("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slides 8 and 12 (script), outline Module 5. The Pikesville mash bill.**
   Current, script 8: "it is widely reported as 51 percent rye, 39 percent corn and 10 percent malted barley, which is right at the legal minimum of rye." Script 12: "A rye that is 51 percent rye and nearly 40 percent corn is closer in recipe to a high-rye bourbon..." Outline: "widely reported as 51% rye, 39% corn, 10% malted barley, the same as Rittenhouse."
   Proposed, script 8: "it is widely reported as 51 percent rye, which is right at the legal minimum." Script 12: "A rye that is only just over half rye is closer in recipe to a high-rye bourbon..." Outline: keep the figure only if the cited sources are re-read and agree, otherwise "reported as 51% rye; corn and barley figures vary between sources".
   Why: the reviewer believes the figure most often reported is 51/35/14 and calls 39/10 doubtful; the slide and deck.json already say only "51%" (R should-fix 3).

2. **Slide 11 (slide row) and slide 11 (script). Rittenhouse date.**
   Current, slide: "Pikesville (from 1982), Rittenhouse (from 1999)". Script: "and in 1999 it bought Rittenhouse".
   Proposed, slide: "Pikesville (from 1982), Rittenhouse (from the 1990s)". Script: "and in 1999, by most accounts, it bought Rittenhouse".
   Why: the outline records a conflict (Distiller says 1993) (R should-fix 4).

3. **Slide 9 (script).**
   Current: "and it hit rye harder than bourbon." and "the old rye distilleries in Pennsylvania and Maryland were starting from further back than most."
   Proposed: add a source to Module 6, or "and, as it's usually told, it hit rye harder than bourbon." and cut the second clause.
   Why: not in the outline (R should-fix 5).

4. **Slide 10 (script), last paragraph.**
   Current: "The turn came in the 2000s, when bartenders started making the old cocktails again, from recipes written for rye."
   Proposed: "The usual account is that the turn came in the 2000s, when bartenders started making the old cocktails again, from recipes written for rye." Or add a source to Module 10.
   Why: Module 10 gives no source for the cocktail-revival cause (R should-fix 5).

5. **Slide 13 (script). Old Forester Rye's origin.**
   Current: "which it says is based on a recipe the company acquired in 1940 with a brand called Normandy Rye."
   Proposed: add the Old Forester source for this to the outline's pour sources, or cut the clause: "Brown-Forman publishes the recipe: 65 percent rye, 20 percent malted barley and 15 percent corn."
   Why: not recorded in the outline (R should-fix 5).

6. **Slide 11 (script).**
   Current: "They were cheap and sold in small amounts."
   Proposed: "They were cheap, and by most accounts sold in small amounts." Or add a source to Module 7.
   Why: not in the outline (R should-fix 5).

7. **Slide 10 (slide).** The 2007 entry and the caption.
   Current timeline entry: "2007 · Mount Vernon's rebuilt still house opens." Caption: "...where Michter's was made until 1989."
   Proposed: replace the 2007 entry with "1989 · Bomberger's closes." (or "1942 · War Production Board"), and caption "...where Michter's was made until about 1989."
   Why: the script never mentions 2007 on this slide, it sits oddly under "Rye leaves the east after Repeal", and the script hedges the closing year ("one account says 1990") (R should-fix 9). The caption is a credit line; its wording is kept word for word until approved.

8. **Slide 15 (slide panel).**
   Current: "Under the 2015 settlement its labels say “distilled in Indiana”."
   Proposed: "Under the 2015 settlement its labels had to say “distilled in Indiana”."
   Why: Templeton now distils in Iowa as well (R minor 9).

9. **Slide 3 (slide body).**
   Current: "A stone still house with five followed."
   Proposed: "A stone still house with five stills followed."
   Why: "five" what is unclear (R minor 5).

10. **Slide 3 (script), found.**
    Current: "a Scottish farm manager, James Anderson, who had distilled before" and "sold mainly at Mount Vernon itself".
    Proposed: add sources for both to Module 2, or cut "who had distilled before" and "sold mainly at Mount Vernon itself".
    Why: neither is in Module 2's "Nailed down".

11. **Slide 7 (script), found.**
    Current: "The city was a port and a railway hub, and before Prohibition there were a great many Maryland brands."
    Proposed: add a source to Module 5, or "Before Prohibition there were a great many Maryland brands, by most accounts."
    Why: not in the outline.

12. **Slide 2 (script), found.**
    Current: "A farm west of the Alleghenies in the 1790s was days by wagon or pack horse from Philadelphia or Baltimore."
    Proposed: keep, and add a source to Module 1 (Module 1's trap already warns against pack-horse figures; this sentence gives none, so it is low risk).
    Why: not in the outline.

13. **Slide 12 (script), found.**
    Current: "These standards come from the federal rules written after Repeal, now in Title 27 of the Code of Federal Regulations."
    Proposed: "These standards are now in Title 27 of the Code of Federal Regulations." Or add a source for their post-Repeal origin to Module 8.
    Why: the outline cites the current regulations only.

14. **Slide 15 (slide strip and script) and slide 17 (slide), found.**
    Current, slide 15: "A label must name the state of distillation if it differs from the bottler’s address." Slide 17: "Distilled in (state) · Where it was made, if it was bottled in another state."
    Proposed: no change to the slides; optionally, in the script for slide 15: "The label has to name the state where it was distilled; the bottler's address covers that when it is the same state."
    Why: Module 8's trap notes that the current rule (§ 5.66(f)) is worded as a general requirement, and the "only when it differs" wording describes the pre-2022 rule. The practical effect is the same.
