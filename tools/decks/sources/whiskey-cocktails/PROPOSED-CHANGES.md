# Whiskey Cocktails: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/The-Whiskey-Cocktail/`)
with its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything
below is a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/The-Whiskey-Cocktail.md`, "R" plus its item number) and my own read ("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slide 9 (script), paragraph 2. "Both are sweeter".**
   Current: "Both are sweeter than a modern Manhattan."
   Proposed: cut the sentence, or "Both lean further toward the vermouth than a modern Manhattan."
   Why: Byron's No. 1 uses French (dry) vermouth; the outline does not support "sweeter" (R 9).

2. **Slide 12 (script), paragraph 1. Peychaud's shop.**
   Current: "was an apothecary on Royal Street who sold his own bitters".
   Proposed: "was an apothecary who sold his own bitters", or add a source for the shop's address to Module 6.
   Why: the outline puts the coffee house on Royal Street and says the dates of Peychaud's own shop vary; it gives no address (R 9).

3. **Slide 13 (script), last paragraph. Herbsaint.**
   Current: "Herbsaint, made in New Orleans from 1934 as a substitute, is still sold".
   Proposed: "Herbsaint, made in New Orleans as a substitute and sold under that name since 1934, is still sold".
   Why: the outline says it was launched in December 1933 as Legendre Absinthe and renamed Herbsaint in 1934 (R 21).

4. **Slide 2 (slide quote). The quotation drops words.**
   Current: "“Cock-tail is a stimulating liquor, composed of spirits of any kind, sugar, water and bitters.”"
   Proposed: "A cock-tail is “a stimulating liquor, composed of spirits of any kind, sugar, water and bitters.”" (the quotation marks then hold only the printed words).
   Why: the 1806 text reads "Cock tail, then, is a stimulating liquor..."; the outline quotes from "a stimulating liquor" (R 14).

5. **Slide 15 (slide, second paragraph). The bootleg explanation.**
   Current: "The bootleg-liquor explanation is plausible and hard to prove."
   Proposed: "The bootleg-liquor explanation is not proven."
   Why: the script and outline say only "not proven" (R 11).

6. **Slide 6 (slide, green card). Found.**
   Current: the card is titled "Old-fashioned, 1880" and lists sugar and a little water, bitters, whiskey, lemon peel and ice, with "Printed under that name by George Kappeler, New York, 1895." under it.
   Proposed: title it "Old-fashioned, 1880 to 1895", or change the note to "The recipe as Kappeler printed it, New York, 1895."
   Why: the 1880 Chicago mention gives no recipe; the four rows are Kappeler's 1895 recipe. The note says so, but the title reads as if the rows were from 1880.

7. **Slide 12 (slide row). Found.**
   Current: "1870s · Thomas Handy runs the bar and buys the rights to Peychaud’s bitters."
   Proposed: "c. 1870 · Thomas Handy takes over the bar; about 1873 he buys the rights to Peychaud’s bitters."
   Why: Module 6 dates the takeover to about 1869 to 1870 and the bitters to about 1873, and says the secondary sources disagree by a year or two; "1870s" puts the takeover a little late.

8. **Slide 9 (slide body). Found, minor.**
   Current: "O. H. Byron’s Modern Bartenders’ Guide gives two versions of whiskey, vermouth and bitters."
   Proposed: "O. H. Byron’s Modern Bartenders’ Guide gives two Manhattans: whiskey, vermouth and bitters."
   Why: each of Byron's recipes also has gum syrup or curaçao, so "two versions of whiskey, vermouth and bitters" is close but not exact. Optional.

## B. Drink slides and recipes

9. **Slides 4, 8, 11 and 14: put the measures on the drink slides (R 2).** Not done here: the brief says not to move measures between slides. The measures, from the script's **Recipe** paragraphs, that would go on each slide as a short line:
   - Slide 4, Plain whiskey cocktail: "1 sugar cube (or 1 tsp sugar) · ¼ oz water · 2 dashes Angostura · 2 oz Old Overholt Rye. No ice, no garnish."
   - Slide 8, Old Fashioned: "1 sugar cube (or ¼ oz simple syrup) · a barspoon of water · 2 dashes Angostura · 2 oz Old Forester 86 Proof · one large piece of ice · orange peel."
   - Slide 11, Manhattan: "2 oz Wild Turkey 101 Rye · 1 oz sweet vermouth · 2 dashes Angostura. Stir with ice, strain into a chilled coupe. Cherry or lemon peel."
   - Slide 14, Sazerac: "1 sugar cube (or ¼ oz simple syrup) · 3 dashes Peychaud’s (1 dash Angostura optional) · 2 oz Sazerac Rye · about ¼ tsp absinthe or Herbsaint to rinse · lemon peel, discarded."
   Why: the room makes the drinks; at present the measures first appear on screen at slide 17. Each drink slide has room for one line under the note.

10. **Slide 8 (script). Orange or lemon.**
    Proposed: add after the recipe: "Orange peel is the modern habit. Use lemon if you want Kappeler's 1895 version."
    Why: every historical recipe the deck cites uses lemon (R 22).

11. **Drink slides: a question for the room (found).** The pilot's pour slides have no "Ask the room" strip and neither does this deck. If the owner wants one, a question per drink would be new wording, for example slide 4 "What changes when the ice goes in?" Not added. (Superseded, November 2026: the owner does not want questions or other callouts on slides. A question for the room goes in the script as an `_Ask: "..."_` direction.)
