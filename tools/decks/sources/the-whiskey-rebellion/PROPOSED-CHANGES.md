# The Whiskey Rebellion: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/The-Whiskey-Rebellion/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below is
a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/The-Whiskey-Rebellion.md`; "R B" for its blocking item, "R S" for should-fix, "R M"
for its minor list) and my own read of the script against the slides and the outline ("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## 0. The owner's decision first: topic and pours (R B1)

The review's one blocking item is not a wording fix. None of the four pours comes from the place or the
people in the story, all four are made in Kentucky, and the script says so itself at slide 14 ("none of
tonight's three ryes is made in Pennsylvania or Maryland any more"). History as the topic is also what
the owner has called beginner material, and slide 17 explains straight and bottled in bond, which the
owner said enthusiasts already know.

The review suggests putting Pennsylvania-made ryes that belong to the story in the glass, each to be
checked for current availability, label and proof, with the date of the check: **Wigle Whiskey**
(Pittsburgh, named after Philip Wigle, convicted on slide 12), **Liberty Pole Spirits** (Washington,
PA), **West Overton** (distilling again at the Overholt birthplace), **Dad's Hat Pennsylvania Rye**;
keep one Kentucky "heritage name" rye (Overholt or Michter's) as "where the style went". Then drop
slide 17 or turn it into "what Monongahela rye was". None of these bottles was checked for this remake;
nothing here is in the outline. The owner should also decide whether this topic belongs in the
catalogue at all. Items 1 to 33 below assume the current pours stay.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slide 1 (hook). Washington's part.**
   Current: "How a federal tax on whiskey, passed in 1791, ended with Washington marching an army into western Pennsylvania."
   Proposed: "How a federal tax on whiskey, passed in 1791, ended with 13,000 militia marching over the Alleghenies, and nobody there to fight."
   Why: slide 11 and the script say Washington went only as far as Bedford and handed command to Henry Lee (R S3).

2. **Slide 1 (script). The presidential superlative.**
   Current: "it includes the only time a sitting president has ridden out with an army."
   Proposed: "it includes the only time a sitting president has led an army in the field himself, as it's usually told."
   Why: the outline's Module 5 lists it as nailed down but cites no source for it, and Madison rode out to Bladensburg in 1814 while in office. Add a source to Module 5 (Mount Vernon is likely to have one) (R S2).

3. **Slide 12 (slide row). The 1795 row.**
   Current: "1795 · About 20 prisoners are taken to Philadelphia. Two are convicted of treason and sentenced to hang."
   Proposed: two rows, "Dec 1794 · About 20 prisoners are marched to Philadelphia." and "1795 · Two are convicted of treason and sentenced to hang."
   Why: the script and Module 5 say the prisoners arrived around Christmas 1794 (R S7).

4. **Slide 14 (script). When Pennsylvania rye ended.**
   Current: "The rye tradition there outlived the rebellion by more than a century; Prohibition and the slump in rye drinking afterwards ended it."
   Proposed: "The rye tradition there outlived the rebellion by more than a century; Prohibition and the slump in rye afterwards nearly ended it. Michter's, one of the last, closed in 1989."
   Why: the paragraph before says Michter's distilled in Pennsylvania until 1989, and "ended it" after Prohibition is not in the outline. Add a source to the pours section (R S6).

5. **Slide 5 (slide note) and deck.json pour 1 `notes`. Overholt's date.**
   Current: "A brand that began in Westmoreland County, one of the four counties at the centre of the rebellion, in the years after 1794."
   Proposed: "A brand that began in Westmoreland County, one of the four counties at the centre of the rebellion, in 1810."
   Why: the script and the outline give 1810, sixteen years later; "in the years after 1794" reads as closer than it was (R M9).

6. **Slide 4 (photo credit). "Sacks of rye".**
   Current: "Men at a farm still, with sacks of rye."
   Proposed: "Men at a farm still, with sacks of grain."
   Why: the Commons title does not say what is in the sacks (R M4).

7. **Slide 17 (slide and script). Bottled in bond.**
   Current, slide: "One distiller, one season, at least four years, 100 proof." Script: ""Bottled in bond" means one distillery, one season, at least four years, and exactly 100 proof."
   Proposed, slide: "One distiller at one distillery, one season, at least four years, 100 proof." Script: "means one distiller at one distillery, one distilling season, at least four years, and exactly 100 proof."
   Why: 27 CFR 5.88 says one distiller at one distillery in one season; the slide and script each give half (R M5).

8. **Slide 10 (script). Maryland rye's character.**
   Current: "Maryland rye was traditionally described as a little softer and sweeter than Pennsylvania rye."
   Proposed: add a source to the outline, or "Later writers often describe Maryland rye as a little softer and sweeter than Pennsylvania rye; few early recipes survive to check it."
   Why: not in the outline (R M7).

9. **Slide 8 (slide body), found. Oliver Miller.**
   Current: "a day after shots from the house had killed a farmer, Oliver Miller."
   Proposed: "a day after shots from the house had mortally wounded a farmer, Oliver Miller."
   Why: Module 4 and the script say "mortally wounded"; "killed" on 16 July overstates what is established about when he died.

10. **Slide 9 (slide row and script), found. Talk at Braddock's Field.**
    Current, slide: "Some talked of attacking the fort at Pittsburgh and of a separate western state." Script: "Some of them talked about attacking Fort Fayette, about burning parts of Pittsburgh, and about the western counties breaking away altogether".
    Proposed: add Brackenridge's _Incidents_ (already listed as a primary document) as a source for the fort and the burning talk, or cut them to "Some talked of a separate western state, even of joining Spain or Britain."
    Why: Module 4's "Nailed down" has independence and Spain or Britain only.

11. **Slide 9 (slide row and script), found. Pittsburgh's response.**
    Current, slide: "The town sent out food and whiskey." Script: "agreed to expel the people the rebels objected to, and supplied food and whiskey."
    Proposed: add a source to Module 4 (Brackenridge again).
    Why: not in "Nailed down"; Module 4 names Brackenridge only as a primary document for "Pittsburgh's response".

12. **Slide 7 (slide card and script), found. Neville.**
    Current, slide: "A wealthy Revolutionary War officer living at Bower Hill." Script: "He was a Revolutionary War officer, one of the richest men in the area".
    Proposed: add a source to Module 4 for his rank and wealth.
    Why: Module 4 names him only as "the inspector John Neville" with a house at Bower Hill.

13. **Slide 7 (slide and script), found. "About 300 miles".**
    Current: "about 300 miles" (slide); "about three hundred miles away" (script).
    Proposed: add a source, or "about 300 miles by road" with one.
    Why: the distance is not in the outline.

14. **Slide 12 (slide row), found. Bradford.**
    Current: "David Bradford, the loudest radical, escapes down the Ohio."
    Proposed: "David Bradford, the best-known radical, escapes down the Ohio."
    Why: the script says "the best-known of them"; "loudest" is a judgement the outline does not make.

15. **Slide 11 (script), found. Kemmelmeyer.**
    Current: "it's attributed to a Baltimore artist called Frederick Kemmelmeyer."
    Proposed: "it's attributed to Frederick Kemmelmeyer." Or add a source for "Baltimore".
    Why: the outline gives the attribution only.

16. **Slide 2 (script), found. Import duties.**
    Current: "Most federal money came from duties on imports, which Hamilton thought were about as high as they could go."
    Proposed: "Most federal money came from duties on imports." Or add a source (Hamilton's report of 1790).
    Why: Module 1 establishes that import duties were the main revenue, not Hamilton's view of their limit.

17. **Slide 6 (script), found. Gallatin.**
    Current: "The meeting's clerk was Albert Gallatin, who later became Jefferson's Secretary of the Treasury."
    Proposed: keep, and add a source for the later office to Module 3.
    Why: not in the outline (well known, but the house rule is that every fact is in it).

18. **Pour order, found.** The proofs run 86, 110, 84.8, 94, not ascending, and the script never says so. Proposed line at slide 14: "This one is gentler than the Pikesville, so give your palate a sip of water first." (house default: say it out loud when the order is not by strength).
