# Grocers and Grain: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/Grocers-and-Grain/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes and one standing house rule (no Jameson) were
applied; they are listed at the end. Everything else below is a proposal and is **not** in the deck
yet. Sources: the independent review (`tmp/review/decks/Grocers-and-Grain.md`, "R" plus its section
and item number) and my own read against the outline ("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slide 12 (slide, 1880s card), script 12 and script 14, slide 14 note and deck.json pour 4 `notes`. The brandy-and-soda story.**
   Current, slide 12: "Brandy is scarce and dear in England. Blends with soda take its place."
   Current, script 12: "The fashionable drink for a respectable Victorian gentleman was brandy, often as brandy and soda."
   Current, script 14: "which is how a lot of it was drunk in London in the 1890s."
   Current, slide 14 and pour 4 notes: "as London drank it in the 1890s."
   Proposed, slide 12: "Brandy is scarce and dear. Scotch and soda is ready to replace it."
   Proposed, script 12: "The story usually goes that the fashionable drink for a respectable Victorian gentleman was brandy, often as brandy and soda."
   Proposed, script 14: "which is how a lot of it is said to have been drunk in London in the 1890s."
   Proposed, slide 14 and pour 4 notes: "as London is said to have drunk it in the 1890s." Or add a source for whisky and soda in 1890s London to Module 5 and keep the words.
   Why: the outline establishes only the phylloxera dates; nothing in it sources the brandy-to-whisky-and-soda switch, and its Traps warn against making phylloxera the cause (R should-fix 4).

2. **Slide 1 (hook) and deck.json `description`. Overstated cause.**
   Current hook: "How a new still, a few grocers and a vine pest made blended Scotch the world’s whisky."
   Current description: "How a new still, a few grocers and a vine pest made blended Scotch the world's whisky between 1830 and 1910. Four pours."
   Proposed description (the review's draft): "How a Dublin exciseman's still, Scottish grocers and a run of London salesmen made blended Scotch the world's whisky, 1830 to 1910. Four pours, tasted part by part."
   Proposed hook: "How a Dublin exciseman’s still, Scottish grocers and London salesmen made blended Scotch the world’s whisky."
   Why: the script (slide 12) says to hold phylloxera's role "loosely" and the outline lists it as a trap; the line is also a stock rule of three (R should-fix 7).

3. **Slide 9 (slide). Order and a date.**
   Current order: Walker, Ballantine, Chivas, Dewar. Current Chivas row: "Grocer on King Street, Aberdeen, from 1838".
   Proposed: rows in the script's order (Walker, Chivas, Dewar, Ballantine), and "Grocer on King Street, Aberdeen, from c. 1838".
   Why: the script reads them in that order; the script and outline say "around 1838" (R minor 11). The heading says "grocers" while Dewar was a wine and spirit merchant; the review offers "The first big blenders were shopkeepers" or leaving it.

4. **Script 1. Where the still is in the painting.**
   Current: "The copper pot still is behind the girl on the right."
   Proposed: "The copper pot still is to the left of the girl on the right."
   Why: in the picture the still stands at her shoulder, not behind her (R minor 7).

5. **Script 16. The three-year rule.**
   Current: "that came in 1915, when the Immature Spirits Act required three years in bond".
   Proposed (optional): keep, and add to Module 8's Traps that many secondary histories give two years in 1915 rising to three in 1916, so the host can answer the room.
   Why: the outline supports three years from s.1(1), but expect pushback (R minor 8).

6. **Slide 11 (slide body), script 11 and outline Module 5. "Aphid".**
   Current: "An aphid from North America ..." / "a tiny aphid that came from North America".
   Proposed: "An aphid-like insect from North America ..." / "a tiny aphid-like insect that came from North America".
   Why: phylloxera is not a true aphid (R minor 9, optional).

7. **deck.json pour 4 `origin` and slide 14 Maker. Dewar's.**
   Current: "John Dewar & Sons, Perth".
   Proposed: "John Dewar & Sons (Bacardi), Scotland" or "John Dewar & Sons, Aberfeldy, Perthshire".
   Why: Perth is the founding town; the firm is Bacardi-owned and its home distillery is Aberfeldy (R minor 10).

8. **Script 3. Stein and the Haigs. Found.**
   Current: "Robert Stein, from a Lowland distilling family, had patented a continuous still in 1827 and 1828. His relatives the Haigs used it at Cameronbridge in Fife."
   Proposed: "... The Haigs used it at Cameronbridge in Fife." or add a source for the family tie to Module 2.
   Why: the outline establishes that the still went to the Haigs' Cameronbridge distillery, not that the Haigs were Stein's relatives.

9. **Script 10. Customers ordering by colour. Found.**
   Current: "The Walkers sold their blends as Old Highland Whisky, and customers already asked for them by the colour of the label."
   Proposed: "... and customers are said to have asked for them by the colour of the label." or add a source to Module 4.
   Why: Module 4 establishes the 1909 renaming, not the ordering by colour.

10. **Script 13. Dewar's family. Found.**
    Current: "Tommy Dewar was John Dewar's younger son."
    Proposed: keep, and add a source to Module 6 (the outline gives his dates and career but not his parentage).

11. **Script 16. The Islington case. Found.**
    Current: "In 1905 Islington Borough Council in London had samples bought from local publicans and merchants and prosecuted them for selling whisky that, the council said, wasn't whisky".
    Proposed: "In 1905 Islington Borough Council in London had samples bought, among them "Irish whiskey" at a pub in Hazelville Road, and prosecuted the sellers for selling whisky that, the council said, wasn't whisky".
    Why: Module 8's established case is one purchase of Irish whiskey on 12 September 1905; "publicans and merchants" in the plural is not in the outline.

12. **Slide 15 (slide). The charges. Found.**
    Current: "In 1901 both were convicted of fraud: Robert got 18 months, Walter 9."
    Proposed: "In 1901 both were convicted of fraud and embezzlement: Robert got 18 months, Walter 9."
    Why: the outline and the script both give fraud and embezzlement.

13. **Slide 17 (slide) and script 17. The fifth category. Found.**
    Current: four categories (single malt, single grain, blended malt, blended Scotch) and the age statement.
    Proposed: add "Blended grain · Single grains from several distilleries. No malt." to the slide, and a sentence to the script.
    Why: Module 8 lists five categories in the 2009 Regulations; the label slide shows four.
