# Bottled in Bond: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/The-Honest-Bottle/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below
is a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/The-Honest-Bottle.md`, "R" plus its item number: "R blocking", "R should-fix n",
"R minor n") and my own read of the script against the slides and the outline ("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## 0. The owner's decision first

1. **The topic itself (whole package).** The deck is about the Bottled-in-Bond Act and is marked
   `"difficulty": "Beginner"`. The deck-creation skill quotes the owner: "People who are highly
   interested in whiskey already know about the bond act", and calls history as the topic beginner
   material. Proposed: the owner decides whether this stays as a deliberate beginner title, or the
   story is narrowed so the Act is the vehicle (for example the rectifier-versus-distiller fight told
   through Taylor and Brown, with bottles from both sides). Nothing else here should be polished
   before that call (R blocking).

## A. Facts and hedges

2. **Slide 5 (slide body). The doctors' complaint stated as fact.**
   Current: "George Garvin Brown sold medicines to doctors, who also prescribed whiskey and wanted a brand they could rely on."
   Proposed: "George Garvin Brown sold medicines to doctors. The company says the doctors he called on wanted a brand they could rely on."
   Why: the script hedges it ("according to the company's account") and the outline's Traps say "The doctors' complaint is the company's origin story. Treat it as such." (R should-fix 4).

3. **Slide 12 (slide and script). "Nothing added" for straight whiskey is on the outline's Not confirmed list.**
   Current, slide: "Straight · Grain spirit aged in oak, nothing added." and "A bonded bourbon or rye is also a straight whiskey: four years in wood, nothing added." Script: "a bonded bourbon or rye is also a straight whiskey: at least four years in wood, with nothing added but water."
   Proposed: confirm the additive rule in the full text of 27 CFR 5.23 and 5.143 and move it to "Nailed down"; if it cannot be confirmed, slide: "Straight · Grain spirit aged in oak." and "A bonded bourbon or rye is also a straight whiskey: four years in wood." Script: "a bonded bourbon or rye is also a straight whiskey: at least four years in wood."
   Why: the outline says the claim was removed from slide 16 and the script, but it is still on slide 12 (R should-fix 5). The "nothing but water" on slide 9 is the bonded rule (27 CFR 5.88) and is confirmed.

4. **Slide 14 (slide and script). Prescription whiskey and the bond.**
   Current, slide: "1920 · Prohibition. Prescription whiskey is bottled in bond." Script: "the medicinal pints sold in pharmacies were bottled in bond at 100 proof. So until Repeal in December 1933 it was more or less the only legal American whiskey."
   Proposed, slide: "1920 · Prohibition. Most prescription whiskey is sold as bonded, 100-proof pints." Script: "the medicinal pints sold in pharmacies were mostly sold as bonded, 100-proof pints, though sources note they did not always meet every condition of the 1897 Act."
   Why: Module 6 says it "did not always meet every condition of the 1897 Act" (R should-fix 11).

5. **Slide 10 (script) and outline pours. "Published" mash bill.**
   Current, script: "Its published recipe is about 27 percent rye, against about 10 in the Evan Williams". Outline: "published mash bill about 63% corn, 27% rye, 10% malted barley".
   Proposed, script: "Its commonly reported recipe is about 27 percent rye, so it should come across as spicier than the Evan Williams." Outline: "commonly reported mash bill about 63% corn, 27% rye, 10% malted barley (retailer source)".
   Why: the outline's source is a retailer, not Beam (R should-fix 10); "about 10 in the Evan Williams" is in no source at all (R should-fix 9).

6. **Script claims not in the outline (R should-fix 9).** For each: add the source to the outline, or use the proposed text.
   - Slide 15. Current: "Brown argued against the Act, and the whiskey writer Chuck Cowdery has pointed out the irony of his company now selling a bonded Old Forester." Proposed: "Brown argued against the Act, and his company now sells a bonded Old Forester named after it."
   - Slide 13. Current: "Rittenhouse is an older brand than its maker; the label is based on its packaging from the 1930s, after Repeal." Proposed: cite the BevNET 2014 packaging release (already in the pours sources) in "Nailed down" if it says this; otherwise cut the sentence.
   - Slide 12. Current: "His Solicitor General, Lloyd Bowers, held fresh hearings, and Taft read the record himself and issued his decision on the 27th of December 1909." Proposed: "His Solicitor General, Lloyd Bowers, held fresh hearings, and Taft issued his decision on the 27th of December 1909."
   - Slide 7. Current: "Accounts differ on which of the two did more: Taylor organised the distillers, and Carlisle pushed the bill through." Proposed: "Accounts differ on which of the two did more."
   - Slide 10. "about 10 in the Evan Williams": see item 5.

7. **Slide 9 (script), found.** Current: "And the label has to say which distillery made it, and where it was bottled if that was a different place." Proposed: "And the label has to name the distillery and its plant number." Why: Module 4 gives "the label names the distillery (real or trade name) and the plant number"; the bottling-place rule is not in the outline.

8. **Slide 3 (script), found.** Current: "neutral spirit, which the new column stills could turn out cheaply and in huge quantities". Proposed: add a source to Module 1, or "neutral spirit from the new column stills". Why: not in the outline.

9. **Slide 14 (script), found.** Current: "Bartenders liked having a 100-proof whiskey that was cheap and held up in cocktails." Proposed: "Bartenders liked having a higher-proof whiskey that held up in cocktails." Why: Module 6 says "bartenders' interest in higher-proof whiskey"; "cheap" is not sourced.

10. **Slide 11 (script), found.** Current: "a chemist at the Department of Agriculture named Harvey Wiley". Proposed: keep, and add "(Bureau of Chemistry, Department of Agriculture)" to Module 5's sources, which name only the Bureau. Low risk.
