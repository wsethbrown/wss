# Sherry Cask Whisky

Research outline: Sherry casks and Scotch, 1800s to now

Sherry casks and Scotch, 1800s to now. A guide for anyone who wants to research the subject and build a talk of their own.

---

**A note on sources.** The facts in this deck were checked against the sources listed under each module in November 2026. What could not be confirmed is listed under "Not confirmed" near the end, with what would settle each one.

## Module 1. Britain and the sherry trade

**The question.** Who drank sherry in the nineteenth century, and who shipped it?

### Nailed down

- Sherry comes from the area around Jerez de la Frontera, El Puerto de Santa María and Sanlúcar de Barrameda in Andalusia. The English called it sack in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.
- Sherry was shipped to Britain in cask and bottled there by merchants in cities including Bristol, Leith and London (The Buyer).
- Sandeman: founded in London in 1790 by George Sandeman of Perth (Wikipedia credits George and his brother David).
- González Byass: founded in Jerez in 1835 by Manuel María González; Robert Blake Byass, his English agent, became a partner, most sources say in 1855.
- Williams & Humbert: founded in Jerez in 1877 by Alexander Williams and Arthur Humbert, his wife Amy's brother.
- John Harvey & Sons was a Bristol wine merchant; it moved its main premises to Jerez in about 1970.

- Sandeman, history: https://www.sandeman.com/history/
- Wikipedia, "Sandeman (wine)": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandeman_(wine)
- Grace's Guide, George G. Sandeman Sons and Co: https://gracesguide.co.uk/George_G._Sandeman_Sons_and_Co
- González Byass, our history: https://www.gonzalezbyass.com/en-gb/our-family
- Wikipedia, "González Byass": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonz%C3%A1lez_Byass

- Sherry Notes, Harveys bodega profile: https://www.sherrynotes.com/2014/bodegas/harveys/

### Primary documents

- UK customs and excise returns of wine imports by country, in the Parliamentary Papers (annual trade accounts).
- The shipping houses' own archives and published histories. González Byass and Sandeman both have long-standing company histories.
- Contemporary wine writing, for example Henry Vizetelly's books on the wines of the world and on sherry (1870s).

### Traps

- The peak of British sherry imports is usually given as the 1870s. See Not confirmed.
- The causes of the decline (cheap imitations from South Africa, Hamburg and elsewhere, health scares, fashion) are commonly asserted. Treat any single cause as an opinion.
- Founding dates of the houses are company claims, repeated by reference works. Byass's partnership is also dated 1851 and 1885; the name González Byass is dated 1863 or 1870.

## Module 2. Transport casks and the empties

**The question.** How did sherry reach Britain, and what happened to the casks?

### Nailed down

- Sherry was exported in cask, mostly in butts of about 500 litres, and bottled by British merchants.
- Empty casks were sold in Britain. Whisky distillers and blenders were among the buyers, along with other users of casks.
- Transport casks held sherry for the voyage and while it was warehoused before bottling. They were distinct from the bodega casks used for ageing sherry.
- Whisky Magazine notes that, as demand grew, some distillers and dealers also paid for old bodega butts, so "stayed in Spain" is the rule rather than a law.

### Sources

- The Buyer (above).
- Whisky Magazine, "Mythbusters: the saga of sherried whisky": https://whiskymag.com/articles/mythbusters-the-saga-of-sherried-whisky/
- Ruben Luyten, "Sherry casks in the whisky industry" (WhiskyNotes): https://www.whiskynotes.be/sherry-casks-in-the-whisky-industry.pdf

### Primary documents

- Distillery and blending-house cask books and stock records, where they survive (for example in company archives held by Diageo, Edrington and the Glasgow University Archives).
- Alfred Barnard, _The Whisky Distilleries of the United Kingdom_ (1887), for what distillers said about their casks.

### Traps

- "Victorian Scotch was routinely matured in sherry casks" is widely repeated and weakly evidenced. Casks of many former contents were used, and before 1915 much whisky was sold young.
- How long transport casks held sherry, and whether they were new oak, varied. Check any specific claim against cooperage or shipping records.

## Module 3. The 1915 maturation law

**The question.** When did Scotch start having to spend years in a cask?

### Nailed down

- The Immature Spirits (Restriction) Act was passed in May 1915 (Lords debate 19 May 1915). It required spirits to be held in bond for two years before sale; the minimum was raised to three years in May 1916.
- The Commons debated a three-year minimum with an amendment for two; the Act began at two.
- The current rule is the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 (SI 2009/2890, in force 23 November 2009): matured in Scotland, in oak casks of no more than 700 litres, for at least three years.
- David Lloyd George, Chancellor of the Exchequer until 25 May 1915, wanted far stronger measures, reportedly prohibition, and the Act was one of the compromises.

- Hansard, Lords, 19 May 1915: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1915/may/19/immature-spirits-restriction-bill
- Hansard, Commons, Clause 1, 17 May 1915: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1915/may/17/clause-1-restriction-on-delivery-for-the

### Primary documents

- The Act itself (1915, 5 & 6 Geo. 5), on legislation.gov.uk or in the Statutes.
- Hansard for the 1915 debates.
- The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 for the current rule: at least three years in oak casks of no more than 700 litres, in Scotland.

### Traps

- Lloyd George changed office (Chancellor to Minister of Munitions) the same month the Act passed. Call him Chancellor for the bill's passage.

## Module 4. American law and the ex-bourbon barrel

**The question.** Why are there so many used bourbon barrels, and when did Scotland start buying them?

### Nailed down

- The requirement that straight whiskey be aged in new (unused) charred oak containers took effect in 1938, under regulations made under the Federal Alcohol Administration Act. Sources give 1 March or July 1938.
- The rule remains in the federal regulations.

### Sources

- Straightbourbon.com forum, "Important date", quoting the 1938 regulation: https://www.straightbourbon.com/community/topic/2541-important-date
- Whisky Magazine, "Mythbusters: the changing face of maturation": https://www.whiskymag.com/articles/mythbusters-the-changing-face-of-maturation/
- Bourbon Veach, "A history of bourbon regulations": https://bourbonveach.com/2023/11/13/a-history-of-bourbon-regulations/

### Primary documents

- The Federal Alcohol Administration Act (1935) and the standards of identity issued under it, in the Federal Register for 1936 and 1938. The exact effective date in 1938 should be read there.
- Current rule: 27 CFR Part 5 (ecfr.gov).
- Industry figures from the Scotch Whisky Association on cask use.

### Traps

- The story that the new-barrel rule was written to protect coopers and the timber trade is widely told (often naming Rep. Wilbur Mills of Arkansas) and none of the accounts found cite legislative history. Treat it as a legend.
- Some sources date the rule to 1935 (the Act) or to Taft's 1909 decision; the Taft decision did not require new barrels according to other accounts.

## Module 5. The end of bulk sherry exports

**The question.** When and why did sherry stop arriving in Britain in cask?

### Nailed down

- Spain moved to require sherry to be bottled in the region of production, which ended bulk shipments and the supply of transport casks.
- It happened in stages. The Consejo Regulador encouraged bottling at source from the early 1970s (Fernández-García), and its own history says that at the start of the 1980s it decided wines registered with it could not be bottled outside the production zone.
- Sources split on the year: The Buyer and Whiskipedia give 1981; Whisky Magazine and VinePair give 1986 (Whisky Magazine: the Spanish government mandated local bottling). Master of Malt links it to Spain's entry to the European Community (1 January 1986). No source found quotes the regulation itself.

### Sources

- The Buyer (Module 1).
- Whiskipedia, "What is an oloroso sherry cask?": https://whiskipedia.com/fundamentals/oloroso-sherry-casks/
- Whisky Magazine, "Mythbusters: the saga of sherried whisky" (Module 2).
- VinePair, "The actual business behind sherry barrel-aging": https://vinepair.com/articles/the-actual-business-behind-sherry-barrel-aging/
- Master of Malt, "Shady sherry: the cask seasoning that isn't": https://www.masterofmalt.com/blog/post/shady-sherry-the-cask-seasoning-that-isnt/
- Consejo Regulador (sherry.wine), "History of sherry casks": https://www.sherry.wine/sherry-cask/the-history-of-sherry-casks
- Eloy Fernández-García, "Marketing strategies in the sherry wine industry", EBHA 2005: https://ebha.org/ebha2005/pdf%20papers/Fernandez-Garcia.pdf

### Primary documents

- The regulations of the Consejo Regulador de las Denominaciones de Origen Jerez-Xérès-Sherry and Spanish government orders of the period, in the Boletín Oficial del Estado.
- Wine-trade press of the early 1980s (for example Decanter, Harpers).

### Traps

- The deck shows "1981–1986": the regulator's own "start of the 1980s" and the 1986 date others give. VinePair treats 1986 as a separate Spanish ban on exporting in wooden casks. The text of the rule was not found.
- Some sherry still reached Britain for bottling later, apparently in bulk rather than in transport butts: Wikipedia says Harveys Bristol Cream has been made and bottled in Spain only since 2019. Do not claim that every bulk shipment stopped in the 1980s.

## Module 6. Seasoned casks and finishes

**The question.** How do distillers get sherry casks now?

### Nailed down

- Cooperages in and around Jerez build casks to distillers' orders; bodegas fill them with sherry, usually oloroso or Pedro Ximénez, for a seasoning period, then empty and ship them.
- Seasoning sherry is commonly distilled into brandy or turned into vinegar afterwards.
- A solera cask is a bodega's ageing cask, kept for decades and rarely sold. A seasoned cask is built to be sold to a distiller.
- A finish is a second maturation for the last part of a whisky's life in a different cask.
- Seasoning periods in practice run from about 6 to 30 months (Whiskipedia).
- In 2015 the Consejo Regulador registered a "Sherry Cask" mark and drafted a specification. Certified casks must be seasoned for at least twelve months with sherry from the Jerez-Xérès-Sherry DO; the Consejo's president put the average at about 18 months.
- Paxarette treatment of casks ended in the late 1980s (one source says the SWA banned it as an additive).

### Sources

- SMWS, "Made to order": https://smws.com/unfiltered/sherry-casks-made-to-order
- VinePair (Module 5).
- Master of Malt, "Shady sherry" (Module 5).
- Whiskipedia, oloroso sherry casks (Module 5).
- Wikipedia, "Sherry vinegar" (for seasoning sherry going to vinegar): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry_vinegar
- The Whiskey Wash, "How aging whisky in sherry casks changed in the modern era": https://thewhiskeywash.com/whiskey-styles/scotch-whiskey/aging-whisky-sherry-casks-changed-modern-era/2/

### Primary documents

- The Consejo Regulador's "Sherry Cask" specification (sherry.wine). Secondary sources disagree on the minimum fill level (two thirds or 85 percent); read the text.
- Distillers' published accounts of their cask programmes (Macallan's is the most detailed). Read as company statements.
- Scotch Whisky Association guidance on cask treatment, for the end of paxarette.

### Traps

- "Sherry cask" does not tell you the oak species. Many seasoned casks are American oak.
- Balvenie's malt master David Stewart finished an ex-bourbon whisky in oloroso butts in 1982; it was bottled in 1983 as Balvenie Classic and became DoubleWood in 1993 (The Whiskey Wash; some sources give 1982 as the release year).
- Glenmorangie's finishing range began with a Port Wood finish in the 1990s; Bill Lumsden joined in 1994. Sources: https://thewhiskeywash.com/scotch-whiskey/this-man-changed-whisky-forever-and-youve-probably-never-heard-of-him/ and https://www.thewhiskyexchange.com/p/22596/glenmorangie-port-wood-bot1990s
- Oloroso is fortified to at least 17% (some say 17–18%), too strong for flor, and ages oxidatively. PX is made from Pedro Ximénez grapes dried in the sun. About 90% of Jerez vineyards are Palomino. Sources: Wikipedia, "Sherry" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry ; Difford's Guide, how sherry is made https://www.diffordsguide.com/bws/1290/sherry/production ; Saveur, styles of sherry https://www.saveur.com/article/Wine-and-Drink/Styles-of-Sherry-/
- Seasoning periods quoted by different firms range from a few months to two years or more.

## Module 7. The three distilleries

**The question.** What did each distillery do about sherry casks, and when?

### Nailed down

- Glenmorangie The Original was a ten-year-old until 2024, when it was replaced by a twelve-year-old at 40% ABV (80 proof), matured for at least 12 years in ex-bourbon casks. US allocation from autumn 2024, suggested price $49.99. The Glenmorangie Company is owned by LVMH (Moët Hennessy); US importer Moët Hennessy USA.
- Glenmorangie Lasanta is a twelve-year-old at 43%: about ten years in ex-bourbon casks, then about two years in oloroso and Pedro Ximénez casks (company description, as repeated by retailers). It was once bottled at 46%.
- The Macallan Sherry Oak 12: Macallan's current wording is "predominantly sherry seasoned European oak casks", seasoned in Jerez de la Frontera; older labels and retailers say "exclusively". Colour is "entirely natural" (Macallan). Bottled at 43% in the US; 40% in some other markets.
- Glenmorangie: William Matheson licensed a former brewery at Tain as a distillery in 1843. LVMH bought The Glenmorangie Company from the Macdonald family in 2004 (completed December 2004).
- Macallan: Alexander Reid, a farmer, took one of the first licences under the 1823 Excise Act in 1824, at Easter Elchies. Owned by Edrington.
- GlenDronach: founded 1826 by a group led by James Allardice. Bought by William Teacher & Sons in 1960; mothballed 1996 and reopened 2002 by Allied Domecq; sold by Pernod Ricard (Chivas Brothers) to the BenRiach Distillery Company, led by Billy Walker, in 2008; Brown-Forman completed its purchase of BenRiach, with GlenDronach, on 1 June 2016.
- The GlenDronach 12, "Original": matured for at least 12 years in Pedro Ximénez and oloroso casks, non-chill filtered, no added colour, 43% (86 proof). A new bottle design reached the US in October 2024.

### Traps

- Glenmorangie's account of selecting oak in the Missouri Ozarks and lending barrels to American distillers is the company's own.
- One US retailer (Wine.com) describes GlenDronach 12 as ex-American-whiskey casks finished in sherry casks. The distillery says sherry casks; follow the distillery and the label.
- Some US shop listings give The Original 12 at 43% or 86 proof. Most, and the brand, give 40%. Check the bottle in hand.
- Macallan's ABV differs by market (43% US, 40% UK). Read the label.
- "Sherry bomb" reputations are marketing. Cask recipes change; read the current label.

## The four pours

- **Pour 1. Glenmorangie The Original (12, 40%).** Ex-bourbon casks only; the reference.
- **Pour 2. Glenmorangie Lasanta (12, 43%).** Same distillery and age, last two years in oloroso and PX casks.
- **Pour 3. The Macallan Sherry Oak 12 (43%).** Matured in sherry-seasoned casks from Jerez, mostly European oak.
- **Pour 4. The GlenDronach 12 Original (43%).** Matured in PX and oloroso casks.

Strengths are as on current US labels. Macallan Sherry Oak 12 is the most expensive of the four and usually above $70. Prices vary too much by region to quote.

## Not confirmed

- British sherry imports peaking in the 1870s, and the causes of the decline. Not searched in depth; the script calls both unproven and no figure is on a slide. Settle with UK customs returns in the Parliamentary Papers.
- Ex-bourbon barrels reaching Scotland in quantity in the 1950s and 60s. No source found; the date was taken off slide 6 and the script says it is the usual account. Settle with SWA or cooperage histories.
- The exact rule and year that ended bottling of sherry outside the region (see Module 5). Searched for the rule under several phrasings; found the regulator's "start of the 1980s", 1981 and 1986. Settle with the Boletín Oficial del Estado and the Consejo Regulador's Reglamento of 1979–1986.
- Paxarette ending in the late 1980s or about 1990, and who stopped it. One secondary source only (The Whiskey Wash); the script says so. Settle with SWA guidance of the period.
- Glenmorangie's account of choosing oak in the Ozarks and lending barrels to American distillers. Company statement; the script presents it as such.
- GlenDronach's 1960 purchase by Teacher's rests on one dated timeline (Whisky Antique) and general accounts. Settle with Barnard-era and Teacher's company histories.
