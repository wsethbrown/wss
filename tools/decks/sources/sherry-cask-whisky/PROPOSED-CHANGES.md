# Sherry Cask Whisky: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/The-Sherry-Butt/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below
is a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/The-Sherry-Butt.md`, "R" plus its severity and item number) and my own read
("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Researcher's voice in the read-aloud script

These lines speak as the person who did the research. A buyer reads them aloud as their own
words, claiming searches they never made. Kept word for word in the deck for now.

1. **Slide 6 (script), paragraph 2.**
   Current: "That story is told a lot and I've never seen good evidence for it, so treat it as a legend."
   Proposed: "That story is told a lot, but nobody has turned up good evidence for it, so treat it as a legend."
   Why: R should-fix 2.

2. **Slide 6 (script), paragraph 3.**
   Current: "The big shift is usually put in the 1950s and 60s, which I couldn't confirm."
   Proposed: "The usual account puts the big shift in the 1950s and 60s."
   Why: R should-fix 2. The outline's Not confirmed list already says the date is unsourced.

3. **Slide 9 (script), paragraph 2.**
   Current: "I couldn't find the text of the rule, so the slide shows the range."
   Proposed: "Sources disagree on the year: the regulator says the start of the 1980s, others say 1986, and one retailer ties it to Spain joining the European Community in 1986."
   Why: R should-fix 2 (talks about the slide itself) and R minor 7 (the slide names the European Community and the script never does).

4. **Slide 12 (script), paragraph 3.**
   Current: "It ended in the late 1980s or around 1990; I've seen both dates, and the record of exactly who stopped it is thin."
   Proposed: "Accounts put the end in the late 1980s or around 1990, and the record of exactly who stopped it is thin."
   Why: R should-fix 2.

## B. Facts and hedges

5. **Slide 16 (slide row, slide strip and script). GlenDronach and sherry from 2008.**
   Current, row: "2008 · Billy Walker buys it and turns to sherry casks." Strip: "Since 2008 GlenDronach has been sold as a sherry-cask whisky." Script: "...led by Billy Walker, who made GlenDronach a sherry-cask distillery, matured in oloroso and Pedro Ximénez casks."
   Proposed, row: "2008 · Bought by Billy Walker's BenRiach company." Strip: "Today GlenDronach is sold as a sherry-cask whisky." Script: "...led by Billy Walker, under whom GlenDronach leaned fully into oloroso and Pedro Ximénez casks."
   Why: Module 7 establishes only the 2008 sale; nothing establishes that the sherry identity began then, and GlenDronach was known for sherry-cask malts before 2008 (R should-fix 7). Or find a source and keep the wording.

6. **Slide 16 (script), paragraph 3. Found the gap, R minor 8.**
   Current: "In 2008 Pernod Ricard sold it to the BenRiach Distillery Company..."
   Proposed: "Allied's whisky business passed to Pernod Ricard in 2005, and in 2008 Pernod sold it to the BenRiach Distillery Company..."
   Why: the script jumps from Allied to Pernod with no link. The 2005 date needs a source added to Module 7 first (the outline names Pernod Ricard (Chivas Brothers) as seller but gives no date for its purchase).

7. **Slide 16 (script), paragraph 2.**
   Current: "In 1960 it was bought by William Teacher and Sons..."
   Proposed: "In 1960, the date usually given, it was bought by William Teacher and Sons..."
   Why: the outline's Not confirmed list says the 1960 purchase rests on one dated timeline (R minor 8).

8. **Slide 10 (slide). Who finished first.**
   Current: "Finish · Glenmorangie, 1990s".
   Proposed: "Finish · Balvenie 1982, Glenmorangie 1990s" (or "Glenmorangie" with no date).
   Why: the script says Balvenie did it first, in 1982/83 (R minor 4).

9. **Slides 12 and 13 (slides). Seasoning time.**
   Current, slide 12: "Fills it with sherry, usually for a year or more." Slide 13: "Usually one to two years".
   Proposed: "usually for a year or two" on slide 12 and "Usually a year or two" on slide 13; keep the range in the script.
   Why: the two slides, the script ("several months to a couple of years") and the outline ("about 6 to 30 months") all differ (R minor 5).

10. **Slide 4 (slide card).**
    Current: "Sherry filled into a new transport butt".
    Proposed: "Sherry filled into a transport butt, often new oak".
    Why: the script hedges ("often from new or nearly new oak"); the outline's Traps say whether they were new oak varied (R minor 6).

11. **Slide 3 (slide heading and script). Found.**
    Current, heading: "Britain was sherry's biggest customer in the 1800s". Script: "in the nineteenth century Britain was its biggest market by a long way."
    Proposed: keep the heading, and add a source for Britain as the largest market to Module 1; or heading "Britain drank sherry in the 1800s" and script "Britain was one of its biggest markets".
    Why: Module 1's Nailed down list does not establish "biggest", and the peak of British imports is on the Not confirmed list.

12. **Slide 1 (slide hook). Found.**
    Current: "How Britain's taste for a Spanish wine put its casks into Scottish distilleries, and what distillers did when the casks stopped coming."
    Proposed: no change to the facts; consider "put its empty casks into Scottish distilleries", since the casks travelled full and only the empties went to distillers.
    Why: wording only; the owner's call.

## C. The look (owner's call, already applied in this remake as the pilot did)

13. **Orange as a background (old slides 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 17).** The old pour slides were solid orange, slides 6 and 12 had orange table bands, and "1915" and "1981–1986" were set in large orange type. Not done here: the brief keeps all 19 slides (R should-fix 8).

## D. Host kit and close (gaps: not invented, for the owner to supply or approve)

15. **No READ THIS FIRST line** at the top of the notes (the one thing to know before opening the box).
16. **No one-sentence version, no roles, no bottle ideas by role, no swap-ins.** The buyer gets only "put the four bottles out in pouring order". The review suggests, to be checked against current labels before use: any all-ex-bourbon Highland 10 or 12 for the reference; another ex-bourbon-then-sherry finish such as Balvenie DoubleWood 12 for pour two (which also fits slide 10); a fully sherry-matured 12 such as Aberlour 12 or Glenfarclas 12 for pours three and four (R should-fix 6).
17. **The older 10-year-old Original.** Glenmorangie The Original became a 12-year-old in 2024, so 10-year-old bottles are still on shelves. Proposed line for Part One: "The older 10-year-old Original works as pour one if that is what the shop has." Note that slide 11 ("The same distillery and age as pour one") and the slide 11 script ("The two are now the same age") would then not hold (R should-fix 6).
18. **No dated prices.** The script says the Macallan is "often over $70"; the outline gives $49.99 suggested for The Original 12 (2024). No other prices, and no date of checking.
19. **No pour cues** (stage directions such as "_Pour the first glass now._") at slides 8, 11, 14, 17, and no short SET UP direction at the head of the script (the first 24 lines after the rule are the public teaser).
20. **No "Ask the room" question** on the pour slides. The pilot's pour layout leaves that strip out because the wording would be new. Possible questions, for approval: pour 1 "What colour is it, before we change the cask?"; pour 2 "What did two years in sherry casks add?"; pour 3 "Is a whole life in sherry casks a different whisky, or just more of the same?"; pour 4 "Darker or sweeter than the Macallan?".
21. **Sláinte has no closing line** (slide 19; script "Thanks for listening. Sláinte."). The house standard is a short closing thought, a toast. The review's idea: a toast to the merchants who couldn't be bothered to ship the empties home, or to the cask nobody planned for (R should-fix 5).
22. **Running order (notes, Part One).** Current: "slides 1–7 (14 min) · pour one (4 min) · slides 9–10 (5 min) · pour two (5 min) · slides 12–13 (5 min) · pour three (5 min) · slides 15–16 (4 min) · pour four (5 min) · close (2 min)." It adds up to 49 minutes against "about 45" and leaves out slide 18. Proposed: "slides 1–7 (12 min) · ... · pour four (5 min) · slides 18–19 (3 min)" with the other times trimmed to fit (R minor 10).
