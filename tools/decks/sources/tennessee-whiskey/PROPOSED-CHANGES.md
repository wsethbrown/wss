# Tennessee Whiskey: proposed changes for the owner

This deck was remade from the older builder's package (`tmp/deck-packages/Through-the-Charcoal/`) with
its words kept as they were. Only mechanical fixes were made (listed at the end). Everything below is
a proposal and is **not** in the deck yet. Sources: the independent review
(`tmp/review/decks/Through-the-Charcoal.md`; "R B" for its blocking items, "R S" for should-fix, "R
minor" for its minor list) and my own read of the script against the slides and the outline
("found").

Each item: where, current text, proposed text, why.

## A. Facts and hedges

1. **Slide 4 (Green card). Who owned Green.**
   Current: "Enslaved. Later research says he was owned by Landis & Green and hired out to Call."
   Proposed: "Enslaved. Sources differ on who owned him: Call, or a firm that hired him out to Call."
   Why: the outline's Module 2 trap says not to state either owner flatly; the script already says the sources differ. The slide does not (R S5).

2. **Slide 6 (caption) and slide 6 (script). Which man is meant.**
   Current, caption: "Jack Daniel's says it has reason to believe the man on the left is Green's son George." Script: "The Times in 2016 described the man on the left as possibly one of Green's sons."
   Proposed, caption: "Jack Daniel's says it has reason to believe the man in the dark hat, second from left, is Green's son George." Script: "The Times in 2016 described the man in the dark hat, second from left, as possibly one of Green's sons." Or re-crop the photograph so he is the leftmost full figure.
   Why: the leftmost figure in the crop is a half-cut face at the frame edge; the man meant is second from left (R S6). The caption is kept word for word in the remake, as instructed; the crop is the old slide's, so the problem is unchanged.

3. **Slide 14 (script). What differs between Buffalo Trace and the Dickel.**
   Current: "Same proof, and the main process difference is the charcoal."
   Proposed: "Same proof. The charcoal is one of several differences, but it's the one the law makes Tennessee whiskey do."
   Why: distillery, mash bill, yeast, age and warehouses differ too (R S7).

4. **Slide 8 (heading and script heading), found. "The rest of the country".**
   Current: "Tennessee bans making whiskey, ten years before the rest of the country."
   Proposed: "Tennessee bans making whiskey, ten years before national Prohibition."
   Why: several states were already dry before 1910; the outline supports only the Tennessee dates (Module 4), and "ten years" holds against national Prohibition (1920), not against every other state.

5. **Slide 10 (slide) and slide 10 (script). The Schenley date.**
   Current, slide: "Schenley revived the brand at a new distillery in 1959." Script: "it first distilled in 1959."
   Proposed, slide: keep, or "Schenley revived the brand at a new distillery in the late 1950s." Script: "it first distilled in 1959, by most accounts."
   Why: Module 5 gives 1958 (Difford's, the rebuild) against 1959 (Wikipedia, the first mash) (R minor 13).

6. **Slide 13 (slide note) and Module 6, found. What NAFTA said.**
   Current, slide: "NAFTA (1994) and the USMCA call it a straight bourbon made only in Tennessee." Script: "NAFTA, in force from 1994, listed Tennessee whiskey as a straight bourbon that may only be made in Tennessee."
   Proposed: add NAFTA Annex 313's own wording to Module 6 if it matches the USMCA's; otherwise "The USMCA, like NAFTA before it, recognises Tennessee whiskey; the USMCA calls it a straight bourbon made only in Tennessee."
   Why: Module 6 quotes the "straight Bourbon Whiskey" wording from the USMCA only; for NAFTA it says only that it "recognised Bourbon Whiskey and Tennessee Whiskey as distinctive US products".

7. **Slide 13 (script). The branding aside.**
   Current: "Jack Daniel's doesn't put "bourbon" on the label, and has said the charcoal makes it something else. That's a choice about branding."
   Proposed: "... and has said the charcoal makes it something else. That's the company's position; legally it would qualify."
   Why: reads as a sneer; the house tone is warm and fair to brands (R minor 14).

8. **Slide 17 (script), found. Bottled in bond.**
   Current: "Bottled in bond means one distillery, one distilling season, at least four years in a government-bonded warehouse, and bottled at exactly 100 proof."
   Proposed: keep the words; add a source for the bonded rules (27 CFR 5.88 on eCFR) to the outline's pours section.
   Why: the rules are not sourced in the outline.

9. **Slide 17 (script). The Uncle Nearest receivership.**
   Current: "the company has been in federal receivership since August 2025 ... and the receiver is trying to sell it."
   Proposed: keep, and re-check before the deck is published or loaded; add "as of late 2026" if the sale is still open.
   Why: dated to the outline's October 2026 sources; it will go stale.

10. **Slide 15 (script), found. Green in the company's story.**
    Current: "But for most of the twentieth century the company's story was about Jack Daniel and Lem Motlow, and Green wasn't in the tours or the advertising."
    Proposed: add a source to Module 7, or "and the company's own telling credited Dan Call instead" (Module 2: "for over a century the company credited Call with teaching him").
    Why: the tours and advertising claim is not in the outline.

11. **Slide 6 (script), found.** Current: "and when Daniel set up on his own, Green went with him." Proposed: add a source to Module 2, or "and, by the family's account, stayed with him when Daniel set up on his own." Why: not in the outline.

12. **Slide 4 (script), found.** Current: "written by Ben Green (no relation, as far as anyone knows)". Proposed: add a source or cut the bracket. Why: not in the outline.
